Chapter 0

What the lecture is really about

Deng’s work is not one theorem but a program: understand when a many-body deterministic system admits a closed kinetic description, make that description valid on the physically relevant time scale, then connect it to macroscopic PDE.

One physical world, viewed through three cameras

Picture the air in a room. A microscopic camera follows every molecule: straight flight, collision, straight flight again. The laws are elementary and reversible, but the number of coordinates is astronomical. A mesoscopic camera stops caring which molecule is which and records a density of positions and velocities. That statistical movie is governed by the Boltzmann equation. A macroscopic camera zooms out further and sees only fields such as density, bulk velocity, and temperature, governed by fluid equations.

Hilbert’s challenge is to prove that these are not merely compatible stories. One must show that, under precisely stated scalings, the detailed movie actually converges to the statistical movie, and that the statistical movie then converges to continuum fluid motion.

The hydrodynamic arrow was already well developed in important regimes. The missing bridge was long-time control of the particle-to-Boltzmann limit.

What Deng and collaborators achieved

Deng and Zaher Hani first derived the wave kinetic equation for nonlinear Schrödinger dynamics on kinetic time and later extended the justification across the full lifespan of the kinetic solution. Deng, Hani, and Xiao Ma then transferred and greatly rebuilt the long-time architecture for hard spheres, extending Lanford’s short-time result and composing it with hydrodynamic limits.

Why it is significant

The work closes a canonical microscopic → kinetic → fluid chain for dilute hard spheres under precise hypotheses. Just as importantly, it supplies a reusable method: preserve connected interaction histories instead of repeatedly pretending that independence has been restored.

The sentence to carry through the lecture

Nonlinear evolution destroys independence, but it may preserve a richer, controllable geometry of dependence. Deng’s mathematics identifies that geometry—in cumulants, diagrams, molecules, and random tensors—and designs estimates that survive long iteration.

The conceptual invariant across Deng’s work

Microscopic complexity is not controlled by forgetting history too early. The long-time proofs work by preserving structured information—cumulants, interaction histories, diagrammatic dependencies—then proving that most of that structure is small after the correct cancellations and combinatorics are exposed.

The transferable proof paradigm that links the wave and particle breakthroughs.
Long-time Boltzmann derivation — Deng–Hani–Ma

In the Boltzmann–Grad regime, for hard-sphere dynamics, the rescaled \(s\)-particle correlation converges to the factorized Boltzmann state \(\prod_{j=1}^s f(t,z_j)\) on every fixed time interval contained in the lifespan of the strong Boltzmann solution. This removes the short-time restriction of Lanford’s classical theorem.

Hilbert program — Deng–Hani–Ma

On \(\mathbb T^d\), \(d=2,3\), the long-time kinetic limit can be coupled to existing hydrodynamic limits to derive compressible Euler and incompressible Navier–Stokes–Fourier from Newtonian hard-sphere dynamics in specified iterated/scaled limits.

Long-time wave kinetic limit — Deng–Hani

For weakly nonlinear cubic NLS in a large periodic box with random Fourier data, the wave kinetic equation describes the two-point statistics on the full lifespan of its kinetic solution, with propagation of chaos and quantitative error control.

“Resolved Hilbert’s sixth problem” should be read in the precise sense used by Deng–Hani–Ma: the program of deriving classical fluid equations from Newtonian hard spheres via Boltzmann kinetic theory, under their hypotheses and limiting regimes. It is not a claim that every interpretation of Hilbert’s broad sixth problem, every fluid, every interaction potential, or every singular regime has been settled.

A compact notation dictionary

The particle phase variable is \(z=(x,v)\in \mathbb T^d\times\mathbb R^d\) (or \(\mathbb R^d\times\mathbb R^d\) in the Euclidean problem). An \(N\)-particle state is \(Z_N=(z_1,\dots,z_N)\). Hard spheres have diameter \(\varepsilon\). The dilute-gas parameter is

Boltzmann–Grad\[\alpha=N\varepsilon^{d-1}.\]

Here \(d\ge2\) is the spatial dimension; \(N\to\infty\) is the particle number; and \(\varepsilon\to0\) is the sphere diameter. In a unit-volume domain, \(\alpha\) is collision frequency up to a dimension-dependent geometric constant and a typical-speed factor. It is not volume fraction: at fixed \(\alpha\), the occupied fraction is of order \(N\varepsilon^d=(N\varepsilon^{d-1})\varepsilon\sim\alpha\varepsilon\to0\). Thus the gas is spatially dilute but collisionally nontrivial.

The one-particle kinetic density is denoted \(f(t,x,v)\), or \(n(t,x,v)\) in the cited papers. It is normalized so that \(f\,dx\,dv\) is the probability mass (or, under another convention, particle-number density) in a phase-space cell. For \(Z_s=(z_1,\ldots,z_s)\), \(f_N^{(s)}(t,Z_s)\) denotes an \(s\)-particle marginal or correlation function; the precise finite-\(N\) normalization varies by source. The statement \(f_N^{(s)}\to f^{\otimes s}:=\prod_{j=1}^sf(z_j)\), for each fixed \(s\), is propagation of chaos. It asserts asymptotic independence of finitely many tagged particles, not independence of all \(N\) particles at once.

The hard-sphere Boltzmann equation is written schematically as

\[(\partial_t+v\cdot\nabla_x)f=\alpha Q(f,f).\]

Here \(\partial_t+v\cdot\nabla_x\) is free transport and \(Q\) is the local-in-\(x\), quadratic collision operator. In this normalization the mean free path is \(O(\alpha^{-1})\). The hydrodynamic limit is the separate regime \(\alpha\to\infty\), equivalently Knudsen number \(\mathrm{Kn}\sim\alpha^{-1}\to0\). This distinction is essential: the story composes a kinetic limit \((N,\varepsilon)\to(\infty,0)\) at fixed \(\alpha\) with a later rapid-collision limit \(\alpha\to\infty\). Limits in several parameters need not commute, so their order is part of the theorem.

Lecture 1

Hard-sphere mechanics and the Boltzmann–Grad scaling

We begin at the microscopic end of Hilbert’s arrow: a deterministic billiard flow in a \(6N\)-dimensional phase space.

One collision is easy; a universe of collisions is not

Two hard spheres meeting in empty space are a textbook problem: reflect the relative velocity across the contact plane. Nothing random or dissipative occurs. Now put an enormous number of tiny spheres in a box. The local rule remains simple, yet predicting every labeled trajectory becomes useless because each collision changes the schedule of later collisions.

The Boltzmann–Grad scaling chooses a delicate middle world. The spheres shrink so their occupied volume tends to zero, but their number rises quickly enough that a typical particle still suffers order-one collisions in order-one time. Imagine a moving sphere sweeping out a thin tube: the tube’s cross-sectional area is of order \(\varepsilon^{d-1}\), and there are \(N\) possible partners. Keeping \(N\varepsilon^{d-1}\) finite keeps the expected collision count finite.

What to picture

A nearly empty room containing so many microscopic billiard balls that each ball still meets a few others. “Dilute” refers to occupied volume, not to the absence of collisions.

Why this chapter matters

Every later statistical object must remain faithful to this exact reversible dynamics. The kinetic equation is credible only if its collision rate, conservation laws, and geometry can be traced back to this billiard picture.

A useful paradox

The individual rule is completely reversible, while the eventual kinetic description has an arrow of time. The mathematics must explain how irreversibility enters through a singular statistical limit—not smuggle it into the microscopic law.

Hard-sphere phase space

For diameter \(\varepsilon>0\) and \(N\) identical particles in \(\Omega=\mathbb R^d\) or \(\mathbb T^d\), write \(z_i=(x_i,v_i)\). The admissible phase space is

\[\mathcal D_N^\varepsilon=\{Z_N=(z_1,\dots,z_N): |x_i-x_j|\ge \varepsilon\ \forall i\ne j\}.\]

Between collisions, \(\dot x_i=v_i\), \(\dot v_i=0\). At a binary collision, define \(\omega=(x_i-x_j)/\varepsilon\in\mathbb S^{d-1}\) and relative velocity \(g=v_i-v_j\). The post-collisional velocities are

\[v_i'=v_i-(g\cdot\omega)\omega,\qquad v_j'=v_j+(g\cdot\omega)\omega.\]elastic reflection
Momentum and energy conservation
complete proof

Momentum is immediate: \(v_i'+v_j'=v_i+v_j\). Write center-of-mass and relative velocities as \(V=(v_i+v_j)/2\), \(g=v_i-v_j\). Then \(g'=g-2(g\cdot\omega)\omega\), which is the orthogonal reflection of \(g\) across \(\omega^\perp\); hence \(|g'|=|g|\). Since

\[|v_i|^2+|v_j|^2=2|V|^2+\frac12|g|^2,\]

kinetic energy is conserved.

Why the collision map preserves volume
complete proof

In \((V,g)\) coordinates the collision is \((V,g)\mapsto (V,R_\omega g)\), where \(R_\omega=I-2\omega\otimes\omega\) is orthogonal. Thus \(|\det R_\omega|=1\). Free streaming is affine with determinant one. Concatenating free flights and collision reflections therefore preserves Liouville volume, away from the measure-zero set of simultaneous/grazing collisions.

The collision law is deterministic and reversible; statistical irreversibility enters only after a singular many-particle limit.

Why \(N\varepsilon^{d-1}\), not \(N\varepsilon^d\)?

The collision rate is controlled by cross-sectional area, not occupied volume. In dimension \(d\), a moving sphere sweeps out a collision tube of volume \(\asymp \varepsilon^{d-1}|g|\,dt\). With number density \(\asymp N\), the expected number of collision partners in time \(dt\) is \(\asymp N\varepsilon^{d-1}|g|dt\).

Mean free path scaling
derivation

For typical speed \(|g|\sim 1\), the collision frequency satisfies \(\nu_{\rm coll}\sim N\varepsilon^{d-1}\). Hence mean free time and mean free path scale like

\[\tau_{\rm mfp}\sim \ell_{\rm mfp}\sim (N\varepsilon^{d-1})^{-1}.\]

To obtain a nontrivial kinetic equation on \(O(1)\) times, hold

\[N\varepsilon^{d-1}\to \alpha\in(0,\infty).\]

Meanwhile the occupied volume fraction is \(N\varepsilon^d=(N\varepsilon^{d-1})\varepsilon\to0\): this is precisely a dilute-gas limit.

One subtle point worth retaining

The gas becomes geometrically dilute while maintaining an \(O(1)\) collision rate. This separation—vanishing volume fraction but finite collision frequency—is the singular mechanism that makes kinetic theory possible.

A deterministic trajectory does not itself converge to a smooth density. One places a probability law on initial configurations and studies marginals/correlation functions under the deterministic hard-sphere flow.

Propagation of chaos

A sequence of symmetric \(N\)-particle laws is chaotic with one-particle density \(f\) if for each fixed \(s\), its \(s\)-particle marginals satisfy

\[f_N^{(s)}(z_1,\ldots,z_s)\longrightarrow \prod_{j=1}^s f(z_j).\]

This says every fixed finite set of tagged particles becomes asymptotically independent as \(N\to\infty\).

The crucial issue is whether factorization at time zero remains approximately true at positive kinetic times despite the fact that collisions generate correlations. Boltzmann’s Stosszahlansatz (molecular chaos) is exactly this closure principle, but a proof must quantify which collision-generated correlations are negligible in the scaling limit.

Elastic collision as an orthogonal reflection

Let two equal-mass particles touch with unit normal \(\omega=(x_i-x_j)/\varepsilon\), and write the incoming relative velocity \(g=v_i-v_j\). The hard-sphere rule is

\[v_i'=v_i-(g\cdot\omega)\omega,\qquad v_j'=v_j+(g\cdot\omega)\omega.\]

Hence the center-of-mass velocity \(V=(v_i+v_j)/2\) is unchanged and

\[g'=v_i'-v_j'=g-2(g\cdot\omega)\omega=R_\omega g,\]

where \(R_\omega=I-2\omega\otimes\omega\) is reflection in the hyperplane \(\omega^\perp\).

Conservation, reversibility, and JacobianComplete proof

Momentum. Adding the two post-collisional formulas gives \(v_i'+v_j'=v_i+v_j\).

Energy. Since \(v_i=V+g/2\), \(v_j=V-g/2\),

\[|v_i|^2+|v_j|^2=2|V|^2+\tfrac12|g|^2.\]

Reflection is orthogonal, so \(|g'|=|g|\), proving energy conservation.

Reversibility. \(R_\omega^2=I\); applying the same collision map again returns the original velocities. Time reversal changes \(v\mapsto-v\) and interchanges incoming/outgoing configurations.

Volume preservation. In the variables \((V,g)\), the velocity collision map is \((V,g)\mapsto(V,R_\omega g)\). Its absolute determinant is \(|\det R_\omega|=1\). Free flight is the affine shear \((x,v)\mapsto(x+tv,v)\), also determinant one. Away from the measure-zero set of simultaneous or grazing collisions, concatenating these maps gives a measure-preserving hard-sphere flow.

This elementary reflection is the microscopic source of the symmetries of the Boltzmann collision operator. Every later conservation law is already encoded here.

Cross-section counting

Fix a tagged particle with speed of order one. During time \(dt\), a second particle collides with it only if its relative trajectory passes through a cylinder whose length is \(|g|dt\) and whose transverse \((d-1)\)-dimensional area is \(c_d\varepsilon^{d-1}\). Thus, for number density of order \(N\) in a unit-volume box,

\[\mathbb E(\#\text{ collisions in }dt)\asymp N\varepsilon^{d-1}|g|\,dt.\]

There are three possibilities. If \(N\varepsilon^{d-1}\to0\), collisions disappear and the limit is free transport. If it diverges, the kinetic timescale collapses and one should rescale toward a hydrodynamic regime. Keeping

\[N\varepsilon^{d-1}\to\alpha\in(0,\infty)\]

is therefore the unique dilute scaling in which a particle experiences order-one collisions over order-one kinetic time. Meanwhile \(N\varepsilon^d\sim\alpha\varepsilon\to0\), so excluded volume vanishes. The two facts coexist: many particles, negligible occupied volume, finite collision frequency.

Mean-free-path estimateDerivation

If typical speed is \(v_{\rm th}\) and the collision frequency is \(\nu\sim \alpha v_{\rm th}\), then the mean free time is \(\tau_{\rm mfp}\sim(\alpha v_{\rm th})^{-1}\), hence mean free path \(\ell=v_{\rm th}\tau_{\rm mfp}\sim\alpha^{-1}\). Therefore \(\mathrm{Kn}=\ell/L_{\rm macro}\) is proportional to \(\alpha^{-1}\) when the macroscopic length is normalized to one.

Lecture 2

BBGKY, molecular chaos, and the collision operator

The Boltzmann equation is not guessed from thin air. It is the closed hierarchy obtained when the exact hierarchy of marginals factorizes in the singular dilute limit.

Replace a surveillance film by a census—without losing the collision mechanism

The full \(N\)-particle density is like a perfect surveillance record: it knows where every labeled particle is and how all labels are correlated. A one-particle marginal is a census: it asks only how likely a typical particle is to have position \(x\) and velocity \(v\). The census is vastly smaller, but collisions are inherently two-person events. Its evolution therefore depends on the two-particle census; that one depends on the three-particle census; and so on. This dependency ladder is the BBGKY hierarchy.

The hierarchy is not an approximation. It is exact bookkeeping for information removed by integration. The approximation enters only when one says that, in the dilute limit, the two particles about to meet are approximately independent: \(f^{(2)}\approx f\otimes f\). That closure converts a linear hierarchy into the nonlinear Boltzmann equation.

Why “molecular chaos” is subtle

Independence is plausible before two randomly chosen particles meet, but an actual collision creates correlation immediately. The theorem cannot assert literal independence at finite \(N\); it must prove that the correlations relevant to finitely many tagged particles vanish in the scaling limit.

Why histories enter

To decide whether two present particles are nearly independent, one traces their possible collision ancestors backward. Trees describe intended ancestry; loops signal recollisions and hidden memory.

Conceptual translation of BBGKY

Each marginal equation says: the variables you discarded return as a boundary flux. A hidden particle crossing a contact surface changes the statistics of the observed particles, so the price of forgetting labels is an infinite hierarchy.

Let \(F_N(t,Z_N)\) be a symmetric \(N\)-particle density transported by the hard-sphere flow. Away from the collision boundary it satisfies Liouville transport

\[\partial_t F_N+\sum_{i=1}^N v_i\cdot\nabla_{x_i}F_N=0,\]

with specular boundary conditions on \(|x_i-x_j|=\varepsilon\).

Marginal

For fixed \(s\), define \(f_N^{(s)}(Z_s)=\int F_N(Z_s,Z_{s+1:N})\,dZ_{s+1:N}\), over admissible configurations.

Origin of the BBGKY collision term
detailed derivation

Integrate Liouville’s equation over the unobserved particles. The bulk transport of those variables integrates to a boundary flux because the integration domain depends on the exclusion constraints \(|x_i-x_j|\ge\varepsilon\). For each observed particle \(i\le s\) and hidden particle \(s+1\), the relevant boundary is \(x_{s+1}=x_i+\varepsilon\omega\). The normal flux contributes a factor \((v_{s+1}-v_i)\cdot\omega\). Using the specular collision boundary condition converts incoming states into outgoing states. The resulting hierarchy has the schematic form

\[\left(\partial_t+\sum_{i=1}^s v_i\cdot\nabla_{x_i}\right) f_N^{(s)}=(N-s)\varepsilon^{d-1}\,C_{s,s+1}^{\varepsilon} f_N^{(s+1)}.\]

Thus the \(s\)-particle equation is exact but unclosed: it depends on \(s+1\) particles.

Under factorization \(f^{(2)}\approx f\otimes f\), the first BBGKY equation closes. For hard spheres, write pre/post-collisional velocities as

\[v'=v-((v-v_*)\cdot\omega)\omega,\qquad v_*'=v_*+((v-v_*)\cdot\omega)\omega.\]

The hard-sphere collision operator is

\[Q(f,f)(v)=\int_{\mathbb R^d}\!\int_{\mathbb S^{d-1}} ((v-v_*)\cdot\omega)_+\,[f(v')f(v_*')-f(v)f(v_*)] \,d\omega\,dv_*.\]
Why the kernel is \(((v-v_*)\cdot\omega)_+\)
geometric proof

Fix \(v,v_*\). During a short interval \(dt\), relative motion is \(g=v-v_*\). A collision with normal in area element \(d\omega\) occurs when the relative trajectory crosses the collision surface of radius \(\varepsilon\). The swept cross-sectional volume is \(\varepsilon^{d-1}(g\cdot\omega)_+\,d\omega\,dt\). Multiplying by particle density and invoking Boltzmann–Grad scaling removes the factor \(\varepsilon^{d-1}N\), leaving the flux factor \((g\cdot\omega)_+\).

Gain minus loss

The term \(f'f_*'\) counts collisions whose post-collisional velocity is \(v\); the term \(ff_*\) counts collisions that remove particles from velocity \(v\). Nonlinearity is the price of closing a two-particle collision process with a one-particle density.

Hard-sphere Boltzmann equation
\[(\partial_t+v\cdot\nabla_x)f=\alpha Q(f,f),\qquad f|_{t=0}=f_{\rm in}.\]

Transport moves particles in physical space; \(Q\) redistributes velocities locally in \(x\).

The equation is mesoscopic. It retains a distribution on phase space \((x,v)\), but it no longer remembers which labeled particles collided. That loss of labels/history is precisely where irreversibility can emerge after the kinetic limit.

Microscopic state

\(6N\)-dimensional, deterministic, reversible, label-resolved.

Kinetic state

6-dimensional one-particle density, autonomous, statistical, generally irreversible.

The hierarchy is a bookkeeping identity for lost variables

Let \(F_N(t,Z_N)\) be a symmetric probability density on the non-overlap domain \(\mathcal D_N\). In the interior, particles free-stream, so

\[\partial_tF_N+\sum_{i=1}^Nv_i\cdot\nabla_{x_i}F_N=0.\]

The collision law appears as a boundary condition on \(|x_i-x_j|=\varepsilon\): incoming and outgoing boundary values are identified by the elastic reflection map. Define the \(s\)-particle marginal by integrating over \(z_{s+1},\dots,z_N\) on the admissible fiber. Because that fiber itself depends on \((x_1,\dots,x_s)\), integrating transport by parts produces boundary fluxes exactly when an observed particle touches an unobserved one.

Hard-sphere BBGKY hierarchy — exact structure, explicit convention

For \(1\le s<N\),

\[\Big(\partial_t+\sum_{i=1}^s v_i\cdot\nabla_{x_i}\Big)f_N^{(s)}=(N-s)\varepsilon^{d-1}\sum_{i=1}^s C_{i,s+1}^{\varepsilon}f_N^{(s+1)}.\]

One convenient gain-minus-loss convention is

\[\begin{aligned} (C_{i,s+1}^{\varepsilon}G)(Z_s)=\int_{\mathbb R^d}\!\int_{\mathbb S^{d-1}}[(v_{s+1}-v_i)\cdot\omega]_+\,\big[&G(Z_s^{,i\prime},x_i+\varepsilon\omega,v_{s+1}^{\prime})\\ &-G(Z_s,x_i-\varepsilon\omega,v_{s+1})\big],d\omega\,dv_{s+1}. \end{aligned}\]

Here \([a]_+=\max(a,0)\); \(Z_s^{,i\prime}\) means that \(v_i\) is replaced by its elastically reflected value; and \((v_i',v_{s+1}')\) is the reflected velocity pair. Sign and \(\pm\varepsilon\omega\) conventions differ across texts because \(\omega\mapsto-\omega\) and pre/post-collisional changes of variables give equivalent formulas. What is invariant is the geometry: integrate normal flux over the contact sphere and subtract configurations leaving the observed state from those entering it.

The equation also carries hard-sphere boundary conditions among the first \(s\) observed particles. It is exact for the finite system, but unclosed because level \(s\) depends on level \(s+1\).

Where the factor \(\varepsilon^{d-1}\) comes fromComplete geometric derivation

The contact surface for the relative position \(y=x_{s+1}-x_i\) is \(|y|=\varepsilon\). Surface measure scales as \(dS_y=\varepsilon^{d-1}d\omega\). The transport flux through that surface is \((v_{s+1}-v_i)\cdot\omega\). Summing over the \(N-s\) indistinguishable hidden particles yields the prefactor \((N-s)\varepsilon^{d-1}\), which converges to \(\alpha\) in the Boltzmann–Grad limit.

The hierarchy is exact. The difficulty is not writing it down; the difficulty is showing that, as \(N\to\infty\) and \(\varepsilon\to0\), its solutions approach the much simpler limiting hierarchy without hidden recollision memory. The displayed operator is pedagogical: rigorous formulations must also specify traces on collision boundaries and the precise normalization of \(f_N^{(s)}\).

How a nonlinear PDE emerges from a linear infinite hierarchy

Formally sending \(\varepsilon\to0\) in BBGKY moves the newly created particle from \(x_i+\varepsilon\omega\) to \(x_i\), removes finite-size exclusion, and sends \((N-s)\varepsilon^{d-1}\to\alpha\). One obtains the Boltzmann hierarchy

\[\Big(\partial_t+\sum_{i=1}^s v_i\cdot\nabla_{x_i}\Big)f^{(s)}=\alpha\sum_{i=1}^s C_{i,s+1}f^{(s+1)}.\]
Factorized solutions are exactly Boltzmann solutionsComplete proof

Assume \(f^{(s)}(t,Z_s)=\prod_{j=1}^s f(t,z_j)\). Then

\[\Big(\partial_t+\sum_i v_i\cdot\nabla_{x_i}\Big)f^{\otimes s}=\sum_{i=1}^s\Big[(\partial_t+v_i\cdot\nabla_{x_i})f(z_i)\Big]\prod_{j\ne i}f(z_j).\]

By definition of the limiting collision operator,

\[C_{i,s+1}f^{\otimes(s+1)}=Q(f,f)(z_i)\prod_{j\ne i}f(z_j).\]

Hence the hierarchy holds for every \(s\) iff \((\partial_t+v\cdot\nabla_x)f=\alpha Q(f,f)\). Thus propagation of chaos is precisely the assertion that the many-body hierarchy asymptotically remains on the nonlinear manifold \(\{f^{\otimes s}\}_{s\ge1}\).

Important conceptual inversion

The Boltzmann equation is nonlinear only because the infinite hierarchy has been restricted to factorized states. The exact \(N\)-particle Liouville equation and the limiting Boltzmann hierarchy are linear in their probability densities. Nonlinearity is the price of statistical closure.

Backward pseudo-trajectories

Let \(S_s(t)\) denote free transport of \(s\) particles. Iterating Duhamel's formula for the limiting hierarchy gives a series \(f^{(s)}(t)=\sum_{k\ge0}I_k^{(s)}(t)\), where

\[\begin{aligned} I_k^{(s)}(t)=\alpha^k\!\int_{0<t_k<\cdots<t_1<t}&S_s(t-t_1)C_{s,s+1}S_{s+1}(t_1-t_2)\cdots\\ &\cdots C_{s+k-1,s+k}S_{s+k}(t_k)f_0^{(s+k)}\,dt_k\cdots dt_1. \end{aligned}\]

Each collision operator adds one ancestral particle when the history is traced backward. Choosing which existing particle is its parent produces a rooted collision tree. The corresponding BBGKY expansion has the same combinatorial tree but uses finite-radius hard-sphere flow and shifted contact points.

Recollision

A recollision is an additional encounter between particles already linked by the constructed backward history, or an encounter not represented by the designated tree edge. It creates a loop in the interaction history and invalidates the naive identification of a BBGKY pseudo-trajectory with its Boltzmann counterpart.

For any fixed finite tree, the set of initial parameters that produces an unwanted recollision is geometrically small as \(\varepsilon\to0\). This is the local geometric heart of Lanford theory. The long-time obstruction is that the number and complexity of possible histories grows so rapidly that “small for each fixed tree” does not automatically sum to “small in total.”

Lecture 3

Collision invariants, Maxwellians, and the H-theorem

Before proving Boltzmann from Newton, understand what makes the kinetic equation qualitatively different from the mechanics beneath it.

Collisions erase detail while respecting a few ledgers

A collision can scramble which molecule carries which velocity, but it cannot change the total mass, momentum, or kinetic energy. After many collisions, almost every other feature of the velocity distribution is washed out. The surviving equilibrium family is therefore parameterized exactly by those conserved ledgers: density, mean velocity, and temperature. This is why Maxwellians are Gaussian and why their parameters later become the fluid variables.

The H-theorem turns that intuition into a monotone quantity. It says the Boltzmann description moves toward less distinguishable velocity configurations. But the microscopic hard-sphere movie can be reversed frame by frame. The apparent contradiction disappears when one notices that the kinetic description keeps the coarse one-particle statistics while discarding extremely special many-particle correlations.

Forward movie

Initially uncorrelated particles collide and create weak, highly structured correlations. Projecting back to a one-particle density makes entropy rise.

Reversed movie

Reversing every velocity also reverses those hidden correlations. The reversed ensemble is exquisitely prepared—not a fresh molecularly chaotic state.

Where Deng’s proof touches the arrow of time

The long-time analysis explicitly carries the cumulant terms that record lost independence. Deng notes in the lecture that their relation to Boltzmann entropy is not yet fully understood. The proof exhibits a mechanism of structured correlation growth; it does not claim a complete microscopic derivation of the entropy functional from those cumulants.

Let \(g=v-v_*\), \(B(g,\omega)=(g\cdot\omega)_+\) for hard spheres, \(f=f(v)\), \(f_*=f(v_*)\), \(f'=f(v')\), and \(f_*'=f(v_*')\); use the same shorthand for a test function \(\phi\). Symmetrization under particle exchange and the measure-preserving pre/post-collisional map gives

\[\int Q(f,f)\phi\,dv=\frac14\iiint B(g,\omega)(f'f_*'-ff_*)[\phi+\phi_*-\phi'-\phi_*']\,d\omega\,dv_*\,dv.\]

The factor \(1/4\) averages the two particle labels and the pre/post representations. With another collision-kernel convention the prefactor may change, but the symmetric bracket is invariant.

Collision invariants
complete proof

If \(\phi(v)+\phi(v_*)=\phi(v')+\phi(v_*')\) for every elastic collision, then the weak form vanishes. Taking \(\phi=1\), \(v_j\), or \(|v|^2\) and using conservation of particle number, momentum and energy at each binary collision yields

complete proof

The resolution is not that Newton’s law “contains friction,” but that the kinetic limit plus asymptotic factorization is singular and discards finely tuned correlations required to reverse a typical kinetic state.

Equality in the H-theorem requires \(f'f_*'=ff_*\) for every collision, so \(\log f\) is a collision invariant. Under regularity assumptions, the only collision invariants are affine combinations of \(1,v,|v|^2\). Thus

\[\log f(v)=a+b\cdot v+c|v|^2,\quad c<0,\]

Why only mass, momentum, and energy survive collisions

A function \(\psi(v)\) is a collision invariant if

\[\psi(v)+\psi(v_*)=\psi(v')+\psi(v_*')\]

\[G_P(g):=\psi(P/2+g/2)+\psi(P/2-g/2)\]

\[h\cdot\big(\nabla\psi(P/2+g/2)-\nabla\psi(P/2-g/2)\big)=0.\]

Now set \(a=P/2+g/2\), \(b=P/2-g/2\). Since \(P,g\) range freely, this says \(\nabla\psi(a)-\nabla\psi(b)\) is parallel to \(a-b\) for all \(a\ne b\). Letting \(a\to b\), the Hessian \(D^2\psi(b)\) maps every vector to a parallel vector, hence \(D^2\psi(b)=\lambda(b)I\). Equality of mixed third derivatives forces \(\nabla\lambda=0\) for \(d\ge2\), so \(\lambda\) is constant. Integrating twice gives

\[\psi(v)=a+b\cdot v+c|v|^2.\]

Thus binary hard-sphere collisions have exactly the classical five-dimensional invariant space in \(d=3\): mass, three momentum components, and energy.

The hard-sphere flow preserves phase-space volume and is reversible, while the limiting kinetic semigroup has a Lyapunov functional. There is no contradiction because the Boltzmann–Grad limit discards fine many-particle correlations; reversing a typical kinetic state requires highly non-chaotic microscopic data that lie outside the forward propagation-of-chaos regime.

Lecture 4

Lanford’s theorem and why “short time” was the wall

Lanford’s 1975 theorem gave the first rigorous Newton → Boltzmann limit. Understanding its proof mechanism explains exactly what Deng–Hani–Ma had to replace.

Lanford could follow every short family tree; long time creates a forest

Observe one particle at time \(t\) and run the movie backward. Who could have changed its velocity? Introduce its most recent collision partner. Then ask who previously changed either participant, and continue. The result is a backward collision tree. For a fixed finite tree, one can usually show that the true finite-radius hard-sphere history is close to the idealized Boltzmann history.

The obstruction is not a mysterious flaw in Newton’s laws. It is a multiplication of possibilities. Every newly introduced ancestor may attach to several existing particles; every collision time must be integrated; and rare recollisions create loops that destroy the tree approximation. Short time suppresses deep histories. At longer time, the number of histories grows fast enough that “each bad set is small” no longer implies “the union of all bad histories is small.”

Lanford’s theorem controls shallow histories and excludes exceptional recollision parameters. The long-time problem is to control whole classes of increasingly connected histories at once.
Why the 1975 theorem was still monumental

It established that deterministic hard-sphere mechanics genuinely produces Boltzmann’s nonlinear kinetic equation—not just heuristically. Its short-time boundary then became a precise research target: invent a state description and summation method that remains stable after many collisions.

Let \(S_s(t)\) denote free streaming of \(s\) particles and let \(C_{s,s+1}\) denote the collision operator that couples hierarchy level \(s+1\) into level \(s\). The variation-of-constants formula is

\[f^{(s)}(t)=S_s(t)f^{(s)}(0)+\int_0^t S_s(t-t_1)C_{s,s+1}f^{(s+1)}(t_1)\,dt_1.\]

Substitute the same formula for \(f^{(s+1)}(t_1)\), then continue. After \(k\) substitutions, the \(k\)-collision term is

\[\begin{aligned} I_k^{(s)}(t)=\int_{0<t_k<\cdots<t_1<t}&S_s(t-t_1)C_{s,s+1}S_{s+1}(t_1-t_2)\cdots\\ &\cdots C_{s+k-1,s+k}S_{s+k}(t_k)f^{(s+k)}(0)\,dt_k\cdots dt_1. \end{aligned}\]

Thus \(f^{(s)}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}I_k^{(s)}(t)+R_K^{(s)}(t)\), where the remainder has the same operator chain but ends in the unevaluated \(f^{(s+K)}(t_K)\). Operator order matters: reading right to left, free-stream the initial \(s+k\) particles, remove one ancestral particle at each collision operator, and propagate the survivors to observation time \(t\).

Each choice of the parent hit by the newly created particle indexes a collision tree. Tracing a tagged particle backward, every collision creates one ancestor; the ordered simplex \(0<t_k<\cdots<t_1<t\) records their creation times.

Lanford, informal form

For chaotic initial data in Boltzmann–Grad scaling, \(f_N^{(s)}(t)\to f(t)^{\otimes s}\) for sufficiently short times, with \(f\) solving the Boltzmann equation.

Proof skeleton
rigorous architecture
  1. Expand BBGKY and Boltzmann hierarchies into collision trees.
  2. For every fixed tree size \(k\), compare the microscopic pseudo-trajectory to its ideal Boltzmann counterpart.
  3. Show configurations producing recollisions occupy vanishing measure as \(\varepsilon\to0\).
  4. Control the entire series uniformly by an exponential/analytic norm.
  5. Pass \(N\to\infty\), then sum the convergent series.

Step 3 is geometric; Step 4 is where the time restriction appears.

A collision expansion branches. A crude bound has the form

\[\|\text{k-collision contribution}\|\lesssim C^k t^k\,k!\times(\text{analytic-weight compensation}).\]

With a suitable hierarchy norm one beats some factorial growth, but convergence still requires \(Ct<1\). At long times, the number of possible collision histories is enormous and a termwise tree expansion loses the information that most complicated histories are highly correlated and collectively small.

Deng’s strategic change

Do not expand the whole long interval as one giant collision tree. Layer time, preserve connected correlations as cumulants, and organize the entire interaction history into combinatorial objects whose smallness can be proved structurally.

which converges only while \(Ct<1\). This toy estimate is not the whole Lanford theorem, but it identifies the structural bottleneck: an argument based on absolute summation of every possible collision history pays a fixed combinatorial cost per collision and therefore has a finite radius in time.

A codimension calculation

Suppose particles \(i,j\) have already been generated in a backward collision tree and, after some free-flight segment, we ask them to come within distance \(\varepsilon\). Their relative position has the form

\[x_i-x_j=(x_i^0-x_j^0)+\tau(v_i-v_j).\]

For generic fixed positions and time window, the condition that this line pass through an \(\varepsilon\)-tube around the origin restricts the relative velocity to a thin angular set. In \(d\ge2\) its measure tends to zero with \(\varepsilon\). Grazing collisions and nearly parallel trajectories require separate cutoffs, but remain controllable for a fixed finite tree.

The distinction between local geometric smallness and global summability over histories is crucial. Lanford solved the former and could sum it for short times. Deng–Hani–Ma needed a new representation in which long histories possess enough global structure that their bad sets remain summably small.

Why “apply Lanford repeatedly” is not a proof

A tempting idea is to prove chaos on \([0,\tau]\), then restart the theorem at \(\tau\), and continue. The obstacle is that the microscopic state at time \(\tau\) is not a fresh product state. It contains precisely the tiny correlations generated by earlier collisions. Those correlations may be individually small but they encode a long memory, and a short-time theorem whose hypotheses demand a near-product structure cannot simply forget them.

What a long-time theory must propagate

Not “the state remains product,” which is false at finite \(\varepsilon\), but a quantitative hierarchy of connected correlations whose size and combinatorial organization are stable from one time layer to the next.

This is the point at which cumulants enter. They are not decorative probability language; they are the coordinates in which “almost independent, but with remembered collision history” can be formulated inductively.

Lecture 5

The Deng–Hani–Ma long-time mechanism

This is the technical heart of the Fields-medal story: replacing a short-time tree expansion by a layered cumulant calculus that remembers full collision history while controlling its connected part.

A relay race in which every runner must carry the right memory

The natural long-time idea is to divide \([0,T]\) into short intervals and apply a Lanford-type argument repeatedly. The trap is at the handoff. At time \(\tau\), the particles are not independent anymore: earlier collisions have produced a faint but structured web of correlations. If one restarts from a product law, one throws away the information that determines future recollisions.

Deng, Hani, and Ma change what is passed from layer to layer. The state is not only the leading factorized density. It also contains connected error terms—cumulants—decorated by the interaction histories that created them. Each cumulant is small in measure, but its geometric support can be intricate. The proof succeeds by keeping enough structure to propagate it, then proving that the connected pieces remain quantitatively small.

The breakthrough is not merely chopping time into pieces. It is discovering a richer state space that closes under repeated handoffs.

Partial time expansion

Expose only the new interactions occurring inside the current layer, rather than expanding the entire history back to time zero again.

Molecules

Compress enormous collision integrals into constraint graphs whose connectivity records which variables are genuinely coupled.

Cutting algorithm

Decompose a complicated molecule into estimable pieces while preserving enough structural gain to pay for the number of diagrams.

The deepest conceptual move

The proof abandons the hope that “almost independent” can be summarized by one small norm. It propagates a structured formula for how independence fails. That is why the method can be iterated when a scalar error estimate cannot.

Fix a target time \(T\) inside the lifespan of a strong Boltzmann solution and choose a small layer size \(\tau\ll1\):

\[[0,T]=[0,\tau]\cup[\tau,2\tau]\cup\cdots\cup[(L-1)\tau,L\tau],\qquad L\tau=T.\]

Within one layer, a controlled short-time expansion remains feasible. The challenge is to propagate the right structured error from layer to layer without paying an exponentially disastrous combinatorial price.

Why layers are not merely time stepping

A naive numerical-style iteration would repeatedly discard high-order correlations and then regenerate them, accumulating error. Deng–Hani–Ma instead propagate an ansatz that explicitly remembers correlations created in previous layers.

For random variables, cumulants isolate connected dependence. The standard moment–cumulant identity for particle correlations is

\[f_s(t,Z_s)=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal P([s])}\prod_{B\in\pi}\kappa_B(t,Z_B),\]

where \(\mathcal P([s])\) is the set of all partitions of labels \(\{1,\ldots,s\}\), \(B\) is a block of a partition, \(Z_B=(z_j)_{j\in B}\), and \(\kappa_B\) is the connected correlation on that block. For example, \(f_2=\kappa_{\{1\}}\kappa_{\{2\}}+\kappa_{\{1,2\}}\).

Paper-versus-pedagogy warning: Deng–Hani–Ma's propagated objects are a tailored cumulant-like expansion relative to the factorized Boltzmann profile, with exclusion indicators, truncations, layer labels, and collision histories. The ordinary \(\kappa_B\) above explains connectedness but is not a substitute for their exact ansatz.

Why cumulants are the right coordinates
complete finite combinatorial identity

Moments and cumulants are related by Möbius inversion on the partition lattice:

\[m(S)=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal P(S)}\prod_{B\in\pi}\kappa(B),\qquad \kappa(S)=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal P(S)}(|\pi|-1)!(-1)^{|\pi|-1}\prod_{B\in\pi}m(B).\]

If variables split into two independent groups, every mixed cumulant vanishes. Hence a small high-order cumulant is a quantitative statement that only weak connected dependence remains—exactly what propagation of chaos requires.

The expansion of a cumulant over many time layers produces a graph encoding particles, collision/overlap events, and temporal ordering. Deng–Hani–Ma call the relevant diagrams collision-history molecules. Their point is not pictorial convenience: the graph exposes the exact combinatorial quantity that controls the integral.

Analytic component

Bound the phase-space/time integral attached to a fixed history diagram. Every sufficiently independent geometric constraint contributes a small factor (ultimately a power of \(\varepsilon\), layer length, or a velocity-volume restriction).

Combinatorial component

Prove that every dangerous connected diagram contains enough usable constraints after an algorithmic decomposition. This is the role of the cutting algorithm and the “excess” bookkeeping.

The inversion of difficulty

In Deng’s own summary: for wave kinetics the analytic side is harder because one needs delicate cancellations among Feynman diagrams; for hard-sphere Boltzmann, the combinatorial side is harder because collision geometries produce a richer graph structure.

A useful abstract caricature is this. Suppose a connected history graph \(G\) contains too many edges to estimate directly. A cutting operation removes carefully selected bonds and decomposes \(G\) into elementary components. The algorithm must guarantee two competing properties:

  1. Each cut exposes a variable whose admissible set has small measure, yielding an analytic gain.
  2. The total number of components/cuts does not explode faster than the gains they generate.
Why cycles can create smallness
geometric heuristic

Consider two particles whose prescribed relative free flights must realize two separated near-collisions. After conditioning on the first event, a genuinely transverse second encounter restricts relative velocity to a thin angular set, suggesting an additional small phase-volume factor. A graph cycle can record this overdetermination.

This is intuition, not a lemma from which the theorem follows. Constraints may be dependent, grazing or very slow relative velocities create singular regimes, torus windings create many candidate encounters, and integrations carry nontrivial weights. The molecule calculus and cutting algorithm exist precisely to certify which apparent constraints are independent and how much gain survives every loss.

Periodic complication

On \(\mathbb T^d\), the same two particles can meet repeatedly after winding around the torus; there is no absolute bound on the number of collisions among a fixed set of particles. Deng–Hani–Ma therefore introduce additional “long-bond” analysis and a modified algorithm for periodic histories.

Deng–Hani–Ma long-time kinetic limit — accurate working form

Fix \(d\ge2\), \(\beta>0\), and a finite \(T\). Let the normalized initial density \(f_0\ge0\) have the paper's spatial summability, one spatial derivative, and Gaussian velocity-weight bounds. Assume the hard-sphere Boltzmann solution exists on \([0,T]\) and satisfies a uniform Maxwellian-weighted bound \(\|e^{2\beta|v|^2}f(t)\|_{L^\infty_{x,v}}\le A\). Start the hard spheres from the paper's grand-canonical ensemble with exclusion, under Boltzmann–Grad scaling. Then, for sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\), uniformly in \(t\in[0,T]\) and even for \(s\le|\log\varepsilon|\),

\[\left\|f_\varepsilon^{(s)}(t,Z_s)-\mathbf 1_{\mathcal D_s^\varepsilon}(Z_s)\prod_{j=1}^s f(t,z_j)\right\|_{L^1_{Z_s}}\le \varepsilon^{\theta_d}.\]

Here \(\mathcal D_s^\varepsilon\) is the non-overlap set \(|x_i-x_j|\ge\varepsilon\), and \(\theta_d>0\) depends only on dimension. The indicator is essential at finite \(\varepsilon\): exact hard-sphere marginals cannot factorize on forbidden overlapping configurations. The simpler statement \(f_\varepsilon^{(s)}\to f^{\otimes s}\) follows for each fixed \(s\) because the excluded set has vanishing measure.

Quantifiers: “arbitrarily long” means that \(T\) is any fixed finite time strictly inside the lifespan on which the required strong Boltzmann bounds hold. It does not assert regularity past a kinetic singularity. The theorem uses a specific ensemble and weighted hypotheses; extending the conclusion to every chaotic initial law is not part of the stated result.

Connected correlations are the right state variables

For the standard probabilistic model, given symmetric correlation functions \(f_s\), define cumulants (Ursell functions) \(\kappa_s\) by Möbius inversion on the lattice of set partitions:

\[f_s(z_1,\dots,z_s)=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi([s])}\prod_{B\in\pi}\kappa_{|B|}(z_B).\]

Thus

If two subfamilies of random variables are independent, their joint moment generating function factors: \(M_{A\cup B}=M_A M_B\). Taking logarithms makes it additive. Coefficients of \(\log M\) involving indices from both independent blocks therefore vanish. Those coefficients are exactly mixed cumulants. In the correlation-function formulation, Möbius inversion on set partitions gives the same statement: \(\kappa_s\) vanishes whenever the \(s\)-particle law decomposes across a nontrivial partition.

Consequently \(\kappa_s\) measures genuinely \(s\)-body connected dependence rather than the large disconnected background generated by products of lower marginals.

For normalized marginals under suitable uniform integrability, propagation of chaos is expressed by \(\kappa_1\to f\) and \(\kappa_s\to0\) for every fixed \(s\ge2\). Deng–Hani–Ma do not merely estimate these static objects: their actual ansatz expands errors relative to the Boltzmann tensor product and decorates every connected piece by the collision history required for layer-to-layer closure.

Renormalize history instead of expanding it forever

Write a long interval as \([0,T]=\bigcup_{m=0}^{M-1}[t_m,t_{m+1}]\) with a controlled layer width \(\tau=t_{m+1}-t_m\). A full Duhamel expansion back to time zero produces collision trees whose depth grows with \(T\). The long-time method instead performs a partial time expansion inside each layer and carries the accumulated past through structured cumulant data.

A useful schematic recursion is

\[\mathcal K(t_{m+1})=\mathcal P_\tau\mathcal K(t_m)+\mathcal N_\tau[\mathcal K(t_m)]+\mathcal R_m,\]

where \(\mathcal K\) denotes the family of decorated cumulants, \(\mathcal P_\tau\) transports existing history, \(\mathcal N_\tau\) adds a controlled amount of new collision structure, and \(\mathcal R_m\) contains histories that fail the normal form and must gain a small power of \(\varepsilon\).

Renormalization interpretation

At the end of a layer, one does not reset the gas to independence. One compresses the past into a controlled effective object and proves that the same class of objects is stable under the next layer. This is mathematically closer to an inductive renormalization scheme than to repeatedly applying a local-in-time convergence theorem.

From collision histories to graph integrals

A collision history imposes relations among particle positions, velocities, collision times, and impact directions. Deng–Hani–Ma encode the relevant connected structure by diagrams called molecules. At a conceptual level:

  • vertices represent particle labels or interaction events;
  • bonds encode collision/geometric constraints;
  • connected components distinguish genuinely correlated clusters;
  • cycles/redundant constraints are potential sources of smallness, because satisfying an extra independent geometric relation costs phase-space volume.

To a molecule \(\mathbb M\) is associated an integral \(\mathcal J(\mathbb M)\) representing, after normalization, the probability/weight of configurations realizing its constraints. The analytic task is to prove bounds schematically of the form

\[|\mathcal J(\mathbb M)|\lesssim C^{|\mathbb M|}\varepsilon^{\mathrm{gain}(\mathbb M)}.\]

The nontrivial statement is that every dangerous connected history has enough certifiable combinatorial structure to force a positive net gain after all losses are counted. The paper’s technical notion of excess quantifies this gain; it is subtler than the elementary graph cyclomatic number, but the degrees-of-freedom intuition is useful.

Toy model: extra constraints create small volumeModel lemma

Let \(X\) be uniformly distributed in a bounded region of \(\mathbb R^m\). If a smooth scalar constraint \(F(X)=0\) has \(|\nabla F|\ge c>0\), then the coarea formula gives

\[|\{X:|F(X)|\le\varepsilon\}|\le C\varepsilon.\]

If \(r\) transverse independent constraints are imposed, one expects \(O(\varepsilon^r)\). Collision molecules implement a far more intricate version of this principle, with singular weights, dependent variables, and constraints generated dynamically rather than prescribed in advance.

Why a combinatorial algorithm is needed

Directly estimating a large molecule is inefficient because constraints are entangled. The cutting algorithm recursively decomposes it into elementary molecules for which sharp integral estimates are available, while preserving enough information about how much \(\varepsilon\)-gain should survive.

1. Detect

Find a local configuration whose removal/cut simplifies the graph without destroying the invariant that measures available smallness.

2. Cut

Factor or dominate the large integral by products of integrals associated with simpler elementary molecules.

3. Account

Track every trivial loss and every improved gain, proving that at least one positive power of \(\varepsilon\) remains for an error history.

The role of the algorithm is analogous to a constructive graph decomposition theorem: rather than merely asserting that a graph contains enough structure, it gives a deterministic procedure for exposing that structure in an order compatible with the analytic estimates.

Why this is the conceptual novelty

Long-time failure is not caused by a single spectacular recollision. It is caused by an astronomical family of individually complicated histories. The proof succeeds by proving a uniform structural theorem about all such histories, then converting structure into measure smallness.

Why the torus is not a routine corollary

In \(\mathbb R^d\), a fixed finite set of hard spheres can undergo only finitely many collisions, with a bound depending on the number of particles. On a torus, particles can wrap around and meet again indefinitely, so no analogous a priori collision-count bound exists. The periodic Hilbert-sixth paper therefore introduces new “long-bond” configurations, new elementary molecules, and modified cuts.

The target remains a bound

\[|\mathcal J(\mathbb M)|\lesssim \varepsilon^\theta\]

for pathological histories, but obtaining \(\theta>0\) can no longer rely on a global bound for the number of collisions of a fixed cluster. The replacement is probabilistic-geometric: if an exceptional cluster experiences extremely many collisions, the set of initial data realizing that behavior must itself be small, and the modified molecule calculus extracts that smallness.

Quantifiers matter

The core long-time theorem is best read in this order:

  1. Fix a strong solution \(f(t)\) of Boltzmann on a finite interval \([0,T]\).
  2. Choose hard-sphere initial ensembles approximating the corresponding chaotic initial data and satisfying the paper’s regularity/decay assumptions.
  3. Take the Boltzmann–Grad limit \(\varepsilon\to0\), \(N\varepsilon^{d-1}\to\alpha\).
  4. Then, for each fixed \(s\), the rescaled \(s\)-particle correlations converge in the relevant \(L^1\)-type topology to \(f(t)^{\otimes s}\), uniformly through the prescribed interval.

The phrase arbitrarily long time means: any finite time contained in the lifespan of the strong Boltzmann solution, not a uniform theorem beyond possible breakdown of that kinetic solution. Near Maxwellian equilibrium, where global strong Boltzmann solutions are available, this yields genuinely global kinetic-time control.

The decisive upgrade over Lanford

Lanford controls histories by absolute short-time expansion. Deng–Hani–Ma propagate a structured long-time cumulant ansatz that retains full collision memory while proving connected pieces remain small. The breakthrough is a change in the representation of dependence, not simply a sharper constant in the old argument.

Lecture 6

From kinetic theory to fluid equations

The long-time Boltzmann theorem matters for Hilbert’s program because the hydrodynamic limit sends the collision frequency to infinity; this requires kinetic control over correspondingly long rescaled times.

Frequent collisions enslave microscopic detail to a few slow fields

At the kinetic scale, a point \(x\) still carries an entire velocity distribution \(f(x,v)\). When collisions become very frequent, most distortions of that distribution relax quickly. Only the components protected by conservation—mass, momentum, and energy—remain slow. The distribution therefore stays close to a local Maxwellian whose parameters \(\rho(x,t)\), \(u(x,t)\), and \(T(x,t)\) evolve in space and time.

Euler equations describe the leading motion along this manifold of local equilibria. Navier–Stokes–Fourier keeps the first lag of the rapidly relaxing microscopic modes; that lag appears macroscopically as viscosity and heat conduction. In this sense, fluid mechanics is a slow-manifold theory for the collision operator.

Why long kinetic time is indispensable

If the collision rate is \(\alpha\), macroscopic evolution observes roughly \(\alpha\) microscopic collisions per particle. A particle-to-Boltzmann theorem valid only for a small fixed fraction of one mean-free time cannot be composed with the rapid-collision limit.

What Deng–Hani–Ma complete

Their long-time kinetic theorem supplies the missing first arrow. Existing hydrodynamic theory supplies the second in specified regimes, allowing a rigorous iterated/scaled route from hard spheres to Euler and Navier–Stokes–Fourier.

Do not picture identical molecular velocities

A fluid velocity \(u\) is an average. Even at equilibrium, molecules have a Gaussian spread of velocities of width \(\sqrt T\). Hydrodynamics emerges because the shape of that local distribution is rapidly forced near a Maxwellian, not because every molecule moves in lockstep.

Define macroscopic fields from velocity moments:

\[\rho=\int f\,dv,\qquad \rho u=\int vf\,dv,\qquad E=\int \tfrac12|v|^2f\,dv.\]

Here \(\rho(t,x)\) is mass density (particle mass is normalized to one), \(u(t,x)\) is mean velocity, and \(E(t,x)\) is total kinetic-energy density. Temperature is the centered variance

\[\frac d2\rho T=\int\frac12|v-u|^2f\,dv,\qquad E=\frac12\rho|u|^2+\frac d2\rho T.\]

A local Maxwellian has a spread of molecular velocities of width \(\sqrt T\); hydrodynamic closure does not mean that every molecule at a point has exactly the fluid velocity \(u\).

Exact local conservation laws
complete proof

Multiply Boltzmann’s equation by \(1,v,|v|^2/2\), integrate in \(v\), and use the collision-invariant identities from Lecture 3. This gives

\[\partial_t\rho+\nabla_x\cdot(\rho u)=0,\]
\[\partial_t(\rho u)+\nabla_x\cdot\int v\otimes v f\,dv=0,\]
\[\partial_tE+\nabla_x\cdot\int \tfrac12|v|^2 v f\,dv=0.\]

These are exact but not closed because stress and heat flux depend on the full velocity distribution.

In the rapid-collision limit, \(Q(f,f)=0\) at leading order, so \(f\) lies on the local Maxwellian manifold \(M_{\rho,u,T}\). Insert Maxwellian moments into the exact conservation laws.

Closure to compressible Euler
complete leading-order algebra

For \(M=M_{\rho,u,T}\), Gaussian identities give

\[\int v\otimes v M\,dv=\rho u\otimes u+\rho T I,\]
\[\int \frac{|v|^2}{2}vM\,dv=\rho u\left(\frac{|u|^2}{2}+\frac{d+2}{2}T\right).\]

With \(p=\rho T\), the moment laws become

\[\partial_t\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho u)=0,\]
\[\partial_t(\rho u)+\nabla\cdot(\rho u\otimes u)+\nabla p=0,\]
\[\partial_t\!\left[\rho\frac{|u|^2+dT}{2}\right]+\nabla\cdot\!\left[\rho u\frac{|u|^2+dT}{2}+pu\right]=0.\]

This is compressible Euler for a monatomic ideal gas, with adiabatic exponent \(\gamma=1+2/d\). The algebra completely identifies the closure assuming convergence to a local Maxwellian. It does not prove that a Boltzmann solution stays close to that manifold; stability of the Hilbert expansion and control of its remainder are the research-scale hydrodynamic theorem.

Viscosity and thermal diffusion appear one order beyond local equilibrium. Write, schematically, with Knudsen number \(\delta\to0\),

\[f=M+\delta f_1+\delta^2 f_2+\cdots.\]

Linearizing the collision operator around \(M\) produces \(\mathcal L f_1\). Solving the microscopic component of

\[(\partial_t+v\cdot\nabla_x)M=\mathcal L f_1\]

generates constitutive laws for viscous stress and heat flux. In a low-Mach, near-global-Maxwellian, diffusive scaling one obtains, schematically,

\[\partial_tu+u\cdot\nabla u-\mu\Delta u=-\nabla p,\qquad \nabla\cdot u=0,\]
\[\partial_t\theta+u\cdot\nabla\theta-\kappa\Delta\theta=0.\]

The precise incompressible Navier–Stokes–Fourier theorem also specifies density fluctuations, temperature normalization, mean constraints, and a Boussinesq-type relation. Those depend on nondimensionalization; the display records the dynamical core, not the full theorem statement.

What Deng–Hani–Ma add here

The hydrodynamic limit itself has a substantial prior literature. Their decisive contribution is the missing long-time Newton → Boltzmann arrow that lets those kinetic → fluid theorems be composed with microscopic hard-sphere dynamics.

Scaling reason long-time control is necessary
nondimensional calculation

Write the Boltzmann equation with collision frequency \(\alpha\gg1\):

\[(\partial_t+v\cdot\nabla_x)f=\alpha Q(f,f).\]

If time and length are both measured in mean-free units, set \(\tilde t=\alpha t\), \(\tilde x=\alpha x\). Since \(\partial_t=\alpha\partial_{\tilde t}\) and \(\nabla_x=\alpha\nabla_{\tilde x}\), division by \(\alpha\) gives

\[(\partial_{\tilde t}+v\cdot\nabla_{\tilde x})f=Q(f,f).\]

A fixed macroscopic interval \(0\le t\le T\) corresponds to \(0\le\tilde t\le\alpha T\). Thus \(\alpha\to\infty\) demands control for a diverging number of mean-free times. This explains the obstruction; individual Euler and NSF limits impose more specific amplitude, Mach-number, and space-time scalings.

The null manifold of the collision operator

At each fixed \((t,x)\), the H-theorem shows that \(Q(F,F)=0\) iff \(F\) is a Maxwellian in velocity:

\[M_{\rho,u,T}(v)=\frac{\rho}{(2\pi T)^{d/2}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{|v-u|^2}{2T}\right).\]

When \(\alpha\gg1\), the Boltzmann equation

\[(\partial_t+v\cdot\nabla_x)F=\alpha Q(F,F)\]

contains a fast collision operator. To avoid an order-\(\alpha\) imbalance, leading-order states must lie close to the Maxwellian manifold. The macroscopic fields \(\rho,u,T\) are therefore coordinates on the slow manifold selected by collisions.

Moments of a MaxwellianComplete calculation

Let \(c=v-u\). Gaussian symmetry gives

\[\int M\,dv=\rho,\quad \int vM\,dv=\rho u,\quad \int c_i c_jM\,dv=\rho T\delta_{ij}.\]

Thus

\[\int v\otimes vM\,dv=\rho u\otimes u+\rho T I,\]

and \(\int |v|^2M/2\,dv=\rho(|u|^2+dT)/2\). The pressure tensor is isotropic with \(p=\rho T\).

Take the collision invariants as test functions

For any collision invariant \(\phi(v)\in\{1,v,|v|^2/2\}\),

\[\int \phi(v)Q(F,F)(v)\,dv=0.\]

Multiplying Boltzmann by these invariants and integrating in \(v\) yields exact local conservation laws:

\[\partial_t\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho u)=0,\]
\[\partial_t(\rho u)+\nabla\cdot\int v\otimes vF\,dv=0,\]
\[\partial_t E+\nabla\cdot\int \frac{|v|^2}{2}vF\,dv=0.\]
Closure on the Maxwellian manifoldComplete derivation

Set \(F=M_{\rho,u,T}\). The preceding Gaussian moment identities give the momentum flux \(\rho u\otimes u+pI\), \(p=\rho T\). A similar centered-Gaussian calculation gives energy flux \((E+p)u\), with \(E=\rho(|u|^2+dT)/2\). Therefore

\[\begin{aligned} &\partial_t\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho u)=0,\\ &\partial_t(\rho u)+\nabla\cdot(\rho u\otimes u)+\nabla p=0,\\ &\partial_tE+\nabla\cdot((E+p)u)=0, \end{aligned}\qquad p=\rho T,\]

which is compressible Euler for an ideal monatomic gas.

The rigorous hydrodynamic theorem is harder because one must show the actual Boltzmann solution remains close to a suitable Hilbert expansion around this local Maxwellian. But the PDE being selected is already forced by collision invariants and Gaussian closure.

How viscosity and heat conduction appear

Near a global Maxwellian \(M\), write \(F=M(1+\delta g+\cdots)\). Linearizing the collision operator gives

\[Q(F,F)=\delta M\,L g+O(\delta^2),\]

where \(L\) is the linearized Boltzmann collision operator. Its nullspace is generated by the collision invariants (expressed relative to \(M\)):

\[\ker L=\operatorname{span}\{1,v_1,\ldots,v_d,|v|^2\}.\]

The hydrodynamic component \(Pg\) lies in this nullspace; the microscopic component \((I-P)g\) is damped by collisions and, to first order, is slaved to spatial gradients of \(Pg\): schematically

\[(I-P)g\approx L^{-1}(I-P)(v\cdot\nabla_x Pg).\]

Substitution into the stress and heat-flux moments yields constitutive laws

\[\Sigma_{\rm visc}\sim-\mu\big(\nabla u+\nabla u^T-\tfrac2d(\nabla\cdot u)I\big),\qquad q\sim-\kappa\nabla T.\]

These are Newton’s viscosity law and Fourier’s heat law. In the low-Mach/diffusive scaling the solvability conditions force \(\nabla\cdot u=0\), and the macroscopic limit becomes the incompressible Navier–Stokes–Fourier system.

Spectral viewpoint

Hydrodynamics is a slow-manifold problem. Conservation laws create zero modes of the collision operator; all orthogonal kinetic modes relax rapidly. Euler keeps only the nullspace. Navier–Stokes–Fourier keeps the first correction obtained by inverting \(L\) on its coercive complement.

The mismatch of timescales

Suppose the collision frequency is \(\alpha\gg1\). Hydrodynamic limits require Boltzmann control on an order-one macroscopic interval while the kinetic dynamics experiences order \(\alpha\) collisions. Rescaling to a normalized collision rate turns that same interval into kinetic time of order \(\alpha\). As \(\alpha\to\infty\), any theorem valid only for a fixed small fraction of one mean-free time is useless for composing the limits.

\[\text{Newton hard spheres}\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\to0]{N\varepsilon^{d-1}=\alpha}\text{Boltzmann at rate }\alpha\xrightarrow[\alpha\to\infty]{\text{hydrodynamic}}\text{fluid PDE}.\]

The 2025 Deng–Hani–Ma paper extends the long-time hard-sphere-to-Boltzmann derivation to \(\mathbb T^d\), \(d=2,3\), and composes it with established Boltzmann-to-fluid results. The periodic kinetic theorem is quantitative: its parameters obey a slow joint-growth condition of the form \(\max(1,\alpha)\max(1,A)\max(1,T)\ll(\log|\log\varepsilon|)^{1/2}\). This suffices for an iterated limit—first \(\varepsilon\to0\) with hydrodynamic parameters fixed, then the hydrodynamic parameter tends to its limit. It is not uniform over every simultaneous path.

For incompressible NSF the input is a small perturbation of global equilibrium in the scaling required by the cited hydrodynamic theorem. For compressible Euler it is a well-prepared local-Maxwellian/Hilbert-expansion regime around a smooth Euler solution. The exact regularity, analyticity, and preparation hypotheses belong to those component theorems. The new paper's achievement is the long kinetic bridge that makes these rigorous compositions possible.

Lecture 7

The wave-kinetic mirror of Boltzmann

Deng’s particle breakthrough grew from methods first developed with Zaher Hani for wave turbulence. The analogy is deep enough that it is useful to learn both systems side by side.

Waves do not collide in space; Fourier modes exchange energy in resonant quartets

A weakly nonlinear wave field can be decomposed into Fourier modes, each oscillating almost independently. The cubic nonlinearity weakly couples four modes at a time. Most quartets exchange energy back and forth with rapidly rotating phases, so their net effect nearly cancels. The interactions that accumulate are resonant: they satisfy both a wave-vector balance and a frequency balance.

The wave kinetic equation is a statistical law for how spectral energy drifts through this vast network of resonant quartets. It plays the role that the Boltzmann equation plays for particles. Collision trees become Feynman diagrams; recollision geometry becomes resonance and pairing geometry; positive collision integrals are replaced by oscillatory integrals whose cancellations must be captured exactly.

A resonant quartet is not a literal spatial collision. It is a phase-coherent channel through which weak nonlinearity transfers energy among Fourier modes.
Chronology matters

Deng and Hani first solved the nonlinear wave kinetic derivation on kinetic time, then developed the full-lifespan long-time theory. That wave work supplied the paradigm—time layers, cumulants, diagrams, structural decomposition—that was later rebuilt for the harder hard-sphere geometry.

Consider weakly nonlinear cubic Schrödinger dynamics in a periodic box of side length \(L\):

\[(i\partial_t+\Delta)u=\lambda |u|^2u,\qquad x\in\mathbb T_L^d:=(\mathbb R/L\mathbb Z)^d,\quad \lambda\to0,\ L\to\infty.\]

We use \(\lambda\) for wave coupling to avoid confusing it with the particle collision parameter \(\alpha=N\varepsilon^{d-1}\). The Fourier lattice is \(k\in L^{-1}\mathbb Z^d\) (factors of \(2\pi\) depend on convention), so the number of order-one-frequency modes grows like \(L^d\).

Write well-prepared random Fourier initial data

\[\widehat u(0,k)=\sqrt{n_{\rm in}(k)}\,\eta_k,\qquad \mathbb E\eta_k=0,\quad \mathbb E|\eta_k|^2=1,\]

with independent normalized complex Gaussian modes in the flagship results and a smooth nonnegative input spectrum \(n_{\rm in}\), together with the paper's box-normalization factors. The observable is not the complex amplitude itself—its random phase averages away—but the two-point function

\[n_L(t,k):=\mathbb E|\widehat u(t,k)|^2.\]

The limiting \(n(\tau,k)\) is a continuum density in wave-number space, with slow kinetic time \(\tau=t/T_{\rm kin}\).

Why \(T_{\rm kin}\sim\lambda^{-2}\)
formal scaling derivation

Pass to the interaction representation \(a_k(t)=e^{it|k|^2}\widehat u_k(t)\). Then \(\dot a_k=O(\lambda)\) is a cubic oscillatory sum. Expanding \(a_k=a_k^{(0)}+\lambda a_k^{(1)}+\lambda^2a_k^{(2)}+\cdots\) gives

\[\mathbb E|a_k|^2=\mathbb E|a_k^{(0)}|^2+2\lambda\Re\mathbb E(\overline{a_k^{(0)}}a_k^{(1)})+O(\lambda^2).\]

For centered gauge-invariant Gaussian data, Wick pairing shows that the order-\(\lambda\) term is either zero or a purely imaginary frequency renormalization and hence contributes no secular change to the real spectrum. At second order, pairing two interaction vertices produces a term proportional to \(\lambda^2t\) after long-time resonance averaging. Therefore order-one spectral evolution occurs when \(\lambda^2t=O(1)\), i.e. \(T_{\rm kin}\asymp\lambda^{-2}\).

This is a formal power-counting argument, not the derivation theorem: the proof must control the expansion uniformly while \(t\) itself diverges like \(\lambda^{-2}\), simultaneously with \(L\to\infty\).

The long-time oscillatory integral concentrates on four-wave resonances satisfying wave-number and linear-energy conservation:

\[\underbrace{k+k_1-k_2-k_3}_{\Delta k}=0,\qquad \underbrace{\omega(k)+\omega(k_1)-\omega(k_2)-\omega(k_3)}_{\Delta\omega}=0,\quad \omega(k)=|k|^2.\]

Writing \(n=n(\tau,k)\) and \(n_j=n(\tau,k_j)\), the wave kinetic equation has the schematic cubic collision integral

\[\partial_\tau n(k)=\iiint \delta(\Delta k)\delta(\Delta\omega)\,[n_2n_3(n+n_1)-nn_1(n_2+n_3)]\,dk_1dk_2dk_3.\]

The vector delta \(\delta(\Delta k)\) and scalar delta \(\delta(\Delta\omega)\) restrict integration to the resonant manifold. Index order and the overall constant vary with Fourier convention; the later derivation gives one fixed convention and its polynomial form.

Same geometry, different algebra

Boltzmann collisions conserve momentum and kinetic energy for particle pairs. Cubic NLS interactions conserve wave number and dispersion energy across resonant quartets. Both generate a kinetic collision integral concentrated on a conservation manifold.

Iterating Duhamel for NLS produces multilinear expressions in the initial random Fourier coefficients. Taking expectations produces Wick contractions and Feynman diagrams. Individual diagrams can be huge; many have leading-order pieces that cancel only after grouping them correctly.

Analysis of a fixed diagram is comparatively geometric; the combinatorial cutting problem is harder.

Wave problem

For cubic NLS in \(d\ge3\), in the paper's simultaneous large-box/weak-coupling scaling and for well-prepared random Fourier data, the rescaled NLS two-point function at physical time \(t=\tau T_{\rm kin}\) converges quantitatively to the WKE spectrum throughout any fixed interval \(0\le\tau\le\tau_*\) on which the required strong WKE solution exists. Corresponding higher even correlations factorize asymptotically.

The 2021 theorem treated the flagship scaling \(\lambda\sim L^{-1}\) for order-one multiples of kinetic time; the 2023 long-time theorem extended the approximation through the full finite lifespan of the strong WKE solution. “Full lifespan” is conditional on that regular solution and on the stated scaling/data hypotheses, not a theorem for arbitrary random data, every dimension, or singular WKE evolution.

Weak nonlinearity creates four-wave interactions

Consider cubic NLS in a large periodic box, schematically

\[i\partial_tu+\Delta u=\lambda |u|^2u,\qquad 0<\lambda\ll1.\]

Write \(u(t,x)=\sum_k \widehat u_k(t)e^{ik\cdot x}\) and pass to the interaction representation \(a_k(t)=e^{it\omega(k)}\widehat u_k(t)\), with \(\omega(k)=|k|^2\). Then

\[\partial_ta_k=-i\lambda\!\sum_{k+k_1=k_2+k_3}\! e^{it\Omega}\,a_{k_2}a_{k_3}\overline{a_{k_1}},\qquad \Omega=\omega(k)+\omega(k_1)-\omega(k_2)-\omega(k_3),\]

up to box-normalization factors. Momentum conservation appears as the lattice constraint; the oscillatory phase records the energy mismatch.

The first Duhamel iterate is order \(\lambda t\), but gauge-invariant random phases eliminate its secular contribution to the averaged spectrum \(n_k(t)=\mathbb E|a_k(t)|^2\). The leading secular change is second order, hence

\[T_{\rm kin}\asymp\lambda^{-2}.\]

Oscillation becomes a conservation law

Second-order perturbation theory produces factors

\[\left|\int_0^T e^{is\Omega}\,ds\right|^2=\frac{4\sin^2(T\Omega/2)}{\Omega^2}.\]
Approximate-delta limitDistributional derivation

Define \(K_T(\Omega)=T^{-1}|\int_0^T e^{is\Omega}ds|^2\). Its Fourier representation is

\[K_T(\Omega)=\int_{-T}^T\Big(1-\frac{|\tau|}{T}\Big)e^{i\tau\Omega}\,d\tau.\]

For a Schwartz test function \(\varphi\), Fourier inversion and dominated convergence give

\[\int K_T(\Omega)\varphi(\Omega)d\Omega\longrightarrow2\pi\varphi(0).\]

Thus \(K_T\rightharpoonup2\pi\delta(\Omega)\). Long-time averaging selects exact resonances.

When the box size \(L\to\infty\), lattice sums simultaneously approach continuum momentum integrals. The combination of the Kronecker momentum constraint and the emergent \(\delta(\Omega)\) produces the resonant collision manifold of the wave kinetic equation.

Gaussian pairings close the spectrum

Assume random Fourier data whose leading statistics are approximately Gaussian and independent across modes. Wick contractions reduce fourth- and sixth-order moments generated by the second Duhamel expansion to products of two-point spectra. Put \(n=n(\tau,k)\), \(n_j=n(\tau,k_j)\), \(\omega=|k|^2\), and \(\omega_j=|k_j|^2\). After the kinetic rescaling, the formal continuum equation is

\[ \partial_t n(k)=4\pi\!\iiint\!\delta(k+k_1-k_2-k_3)\,\delta(\omega+\omega_1-\omega_2-\omega_3)\\ \times n n_1 n_2 n_3\Big(\frac1n+\frac1{n_1}-\frac1{n_2}-\frac1{n_3}\Big)\,dk_1dk_2dk_3. \]

The parenthesis is compact notation. Multiplying out gives the polynomial \(n_1n_2n_3+nn_2n_3-nn_1n_3-nn_1n_2\), which remains meaningful when a spectrum vanishes. The two Dirac distributions restrict integration to quartets conserving wave number and linear-wave energy. The overall constant depends on Fourier normalization. This equation is the wave analogue of Boltzmann: a deterministic many-mode PDE has been compressed into an autonomous statistical evolution for one-mode energy density.

Where the hard analysis lives

Formal perturbation theory sees the resonant delta quickly. A proof must show that all non-leading diagrams, near-resonant arithmetic effects, repeated interactions, and non-Gaussian correlations are collectively negligible under a simultaneous \(L\to\infty\), \(\lambda\to0\) scaling.

Lecture 8

Random tensors and nonlinear Schrödinger dynamics

The third strand cited by the IMU is Deng’s probabilistic approach to nonlinear dispersive equations, especially the idea that randomness can be propagated through nonlinear expansions with tensor-level operator control.

Randomness is a resource—but nonlinear evolution spends it unless the proof keeps accounts

Independent random Fourier coefficients rarely align in the worst possible way. That typical cancellation lets random initial data behave better than an adversarial deterministic function with the same roughness. But after one nonlinear interaction, the output modes share inputs and are no longer independent. A proof that merely says “the coefficients are random” has consumed its only advantage.

Random tensor theory is an accounting system for the remaining randomness. Instead of compressing a multilinear random expression into one scalar norm, it records which indices are inputs, which are outputs, which may still be averaged, and which may later be contracted against something already dependent. The estimate is chosen for the object’s future role, not only for its present size.

Random tensors enlarge the class of objects that can be propagated: no longer independent variables, but still structured enough to retain cancellation.
Connection to the kinetic work

The common move is to replace a fragile property—literal independence—by a richer property stable under nonlinear evolution. For NLS this is multilinear Gaussian structure with tensor coefficients; for kinetic limits it is connected cumulants decorated by interaction history.

A standard randomized Fourier series is

\[u_0^\omega(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z^d} g_k(\omega)\widehat u_0(k)e^{ik\cdot x}.\]

Here \(\omega\) labels the random outcome, \(\widehat u_0(k)\) is a fixed deterministic Fourier profile, and \(g_k\) are independent normalized random multipliers—standard complex Gaussians in the cited random-tensor framework. Randomization does not improve the deterministic Sobolev differentiability of the coefficient sequence: it improves typical summability and suppresses adversarial phase alignment in multilinear expressions.

The key distinction

Random-data PDE is not “replace the PDE by noise.” The evolution remains deterministic after the initial random draw. Probability is a microscope for typical geometry in function space.

After iterating a nonlinear PDE, a homogeneous degree-\(r\) term may first look like

\[X_{n_{\rm out}}=\sum_{n_1,\ldots,n_r} h_{n_{\rm out};n_1\ldots n_r}\,g_{n_1}\cdots g_{n_r}.\]

The indices \(n_j\) are input Fourier modes, \(n_{\rm out}\) is the output mode, and \(h\) records deterministic resonance, time-integration, and frequency weights. In later iterations the coefficient tensor may itself depend on randomness revealed earlier; “adapted” means that this dependence is organized relative to a filtration rather than treated as fresh independence.

A tensor can be flattened by partitioning its indices into two groups. For example, \(h_{i;jk}\) can be read as a matrix from \(\ell^2_{j,k}\) to \(\ell^2_i\), from \(\ell^2_k\) to \(\ell^2_{i,j}\), or as a scalar array with Hilbert–Schmidt norm. Each flattening predicts a different future contraction. The theory retains a family of these norms instead of paying the generally catastrophic coefficient sum \(\sum|h|\).

Hilbert–Schmidt view

Square-sum all coefficients: robust but sometimes too expensive.

Operator-flattening view

Group subsets of indices as inputs/outputs and exploit independence to gain sharper bounds.

Gaussian pairings as the combinatorial engine
complete finite-dimensional statement

For centered jointly real Gaussian variables \(g_1,\dots,g_{2m}\), Wick’s formula states

\[\mathbb E\prod_{j=1}^{2m}g_j=\sum_{\text{pairings }P}\prod_{\{a,b\}\in P}\mathbb E(g_ag_b),\]

while odd moments vanish. For standard complex Gaussians, apply the same real-Gaussian formula to real and imaginary parts; equivalently, nonzero contractions pair \(g_k\) with \(\overline{g_{k'}}\), giving \(\mathbb E[g_k\overline{g_{k'}}]=\delta_{kk'}\), whereas \(\mathbb E[g_kg_{k'}]=0\). Thus high moments become sums of index identifications. Random tensor estimates control these contraction patterns without enumerating them term by term.

Deng–Nahmod–Yue developed random averaging operators and random tensor theory to treat nonlinear dispersive equations below deterministic regularity thresholds and to propagate randomness through the nonlinear flow. The broader methodological lesson is the same as in kinetic limits: encode dependency structure instead of bounding every term independently.

Cross-domain synthesis

Collision-history molecules, Feynman diagrams, and random tensors are three realizations of one meta-technique: turn a nonlinear expansion into a structured combinatorial object, identify which substructures are genuinely dependent, and let analytic estimates exploit the remaining independence/codimension/cancellation.

Why nonlinear random data immediately stops being independent

Suppose the initial Fourier coefficients are independent complex Gaussians \(g_k\). A nonlinear term such as \(|u|^2u\) produces cubic expressions

\[X_k=\sum_{k_1-k_2+k_3=k}T_{k;k_1k_2k_3}\,g_{k_1}\overline{g_{k_2}}g_{k_3}.\]

The output \(X_k\) is no longer an independent Gaussian sequence. Nevertheless it lies in a finite Wiener chaos and retains rigid algebraic structure. Iterating the PDE produces higher multilinear Gaussian chaoses with coefficients that depend on earlier random objects.

A deterministic norm estimate typically treats all coefficient arrays as worst-case tensors and loses the square-root cancellation supplied by randomness. A scalar probabilistic estimate, on the other hand, may exploit cancellation but forget how the random output will be contracted at the next nonlinear step. Random tensor calculus is designed to preserve both pieces of information.

Estimate an object by how it can be contracted later

For a matrix \(A=(A_{ij})\), one does not summarize its behavior by the largest entry; operator norm, Hilbert–Schmidt norm, and Schatten norms predict different future contractions. A random tensor generalizes this philosophy to multilinear coefficient arrays. One tracks norms associated with partitions of indices, corresponding to different ways future nonlinear operations may group and contract them.

Toy Gaussian contractionComplete calculation

Let \(X_i=\sum_jA_{ij}g_j\) with independent standard complex Gaussians. Then

\[\mathbb E\|X\|_{\ell^2_i}^2=\sum_{i,j}|A_{ij}|^2=\|A\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2.\]

For a deterministic input \(h\), however, \(\|Ah\|_2\le\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|h\|_2\). Thus the same coefficient array naturally carries at least two relevant norms, depending on whether an index will be averaged against fresh Gaussian randomness or contracted against an adversarial vector. Higher random tensors require a whole family of such partition-dependent norms.

This “future-proof” bookkeeping is what allows propagation of randomness through many nonlinear iterations rather than consuming all probabilistic gain in the first estimate.

One theme: preserve structured randomness under dynamics

The random-tensor work and the kinetic-limit work operate at different technical levels, but they share a methodological invariant. Nonlinear evolution destroys literal independence immediately. The goal is therefore not to prove that independence survives; it is to identify a richer class of structured dependence that is closed enough under the evolution and still provides quantitative cancellation.

  • In probabilistic NLS, the closed class is built from multilinear Gaussian objects with random tensor coefficients.
  • In wave kinetics, it is a hierarchy of diagrammatically organized correlations/cumulants.
  • In the hard-sphere problem, it is a long-time cumulant ansatz decorated by collision-history molecules.
A useful meta-principle

When a stochastic or kinetic proof fails because “independence is lost,” search for the smallest enlargement of independence that is dynamically stable. Deng’s body of work can be read as a sequence of increasingly powerful answers to that question.

Lecture 9

What the breakthrough proves—and what remains open

A research-level understanding requires sharp boundary conditions on the claim.

What changed in the field

Before this program, the central microscopic-to-Boltzmann theorem for hard spheres stopped at a sufficiently short time, even when the target Boltzmann solution remained smooth much longer. Deng, Hani, and Ma removed that mismatch: on every fixed interval within the strong Boltzmann solution’s lifespan, the dilute hard-sphere statistics follow Boltzmann with quantitative error. Their companion work then joins this bridge to established hydrodynamic limits in precise periodic regimes.

On the wave side, Deng and Hani rigorously derived the wave kinetic equation for a nonlinear dispersive system at kinetic time and subsequently extended the description through the full lifespan of the wave kinetic solution. The particle result did not arise in isolation; it is evidence that the same long-time language of connected histories can survive across physically different kinetic systems.

Foundational significance

A famous chain from Newtonian particles through Boltzmann to fundamental fluid PDE is now rigorous for a canonical dilute hard-sphere model under specified hypotheses.

Methodological significance

The work introduces a long-time calculus of structured dependence: partial expansions, cumulants, diagrams, molecules, and cutting algorithms.

Cross-domain significance

Ideas developed for wave turbulence were transformed into tools for particle kinetics, suggesting universality at the level of interaction histories rather than equations alone.

What the result does not say

It does not derive every fluid model from every microscopic interaction, solve dense liquids, cross singularities of the target kinetic or fluid PDE, or settle three-dimensional Navier–Stokes regularity. Its importance comes from completing a foundational program in a sharply defined model, with a proof architecture that points beyond that model.

Arbitrary potentials

Beyond smooth lifespan

The theorem tracks the kinetic PDE while its requisite regularity exists; it does not magically resolve blow-up/regularity questions for every target PDE.

Simultaneous universal limit

The Hilbert-program theorem uses carefully specified iterated or linked limits, not a claim that every path in the multi-parameter limit produces the same macrodynamics.

  • Landau equation / grazing collisions: many weak deflections per unit time suggest a kinetic central-limit phenomenon rather than isolated binary jumps.
  • Quantum Boltzmann: identify the correct microscopic model and the mixture of quadratic/cubic collision structures.
  • Inhomogeneous wave turbulence: wave packets add transport in physical space and couple resonance geometry to spatial localization.
  • Quasilinear wave systems: water waves and related models have nonlinearities that affect the highest derivatives, making long expansions far more fragile.
  • Turbulent cascades / condensates: deriving a kinetic equation is not yet deriving Kolmogorov–Zakharov spectra or singular condensate formation from first principles.
A research heuristic

Whenever you see a deterministic many-degree-of-freedom system with weak local interactions, ask four questions: (i) what is the kinetic timescale? (ii) what is the correct chaos/factorization object? (iii) what diagrams encode memory? (iv) what structural operation—cancellation, cutting, renormalization—turns diagram complexity into a small parameter?

A singular limit does not commute with time reversal

At every fixed \(N,\varepsilon\), the hard-sphere flow is reversible. If \(R(x_i,v_i)=(x_i,-v_i)\), then

\[R\,\mathcal H_N(t)\,R=\mathcal H_N(-t).\]

Boltzmann evolution, by contrast, obeys \(H(f(t))\le H(f(0))\). The resolution is not that Newtonian reversibility is approximate. Rather, the kinetic limit is taken on a special class of asymptotically chaotic ensembles. Forward evolution creates correlations of vanishing amplitude but highly organized form. Velocity reversal maps these states to ensembles whose hidden correlations are precisely tuned to produce entropy decrease; they are not generic chaotic data to which the same forward theorem applies.

Mathematical moral

Macroscopic irreversibility can emerge from reversible equations when the limiting projection forgets information and the admissible class of initial data is not invariant under the microscopic time-reversal operation after that projection.

Precise scope of the Hilbert-sixth claim

The 2025 paper itself uses careful wording: it resolves Hilbert’s sixth problem as it pertains to the program of deriving fluid equations from Newton’s laws by way of Boltzmann kinetic theory. Concretely, it supplies the previously missing long kinetic bridge for dilute hard spheres and composes it, in specified periodic and regularity regimes, with established hydrodynamic limits to compressible Euler and incompressible Navier–Stokes–Fourier.

That does not mean that all of the following are solved:

  • general smooth or long-range intermolecular potentials;
  • dense gases, liquids, or solid phases;
  • arbitrary boundaries with boundary layers;
  • every scaling from Newton to every continuum PDE;
  • global smooth compressible Euler past shocks;
  • the 3D Navier–Stokes regularity problem.

The result is profound because it closes a canonical 125-year-old mathematical-physics chain in a foundational model—not because it makes kinetic theory universally rigorous in one stroke.

From equations to universality classes of interaction histories

The deepest portable idea in Deng’s lecture is arguably not any single PDE. It is that kinetic limits can be organized around the combinatorics of interaction histories. Different physical systems supply different local weights—hard-sphere geometry, oscillatory phases, lattice resonances—but the long-time problem repeatedly becomes:

  1. expand enough to expose interaction history;
  2. subtract disconnected/factorized pieces;
  3. classify the remaining connected diagrams;
  4. find a structural invariant certifying analytic smallness;
  5. sum entire diagram classes without paying absolute cost per interaction;
  6. renormalize and propagate the description across macroscopic time layers.

This suggests a research heuristic for other kinetic limits—quantum gases, phonons, weakly coupled fields, plasma models: first ask whether one can build a closed calculus of connected histories. If yes, the “kinetic equation” may be only the visible fixed point of a deeper diagrammatic renormalization mechanism.

Five compressed arguments, unpacked one inference at a time

Here “unpacked” means that every logical move is exposed: the hypotheses, the object being transformed, the identity used, and the conclusion. These are not power-series expansions. The status label distinguishes a complete elementary argument from an outline of a research-scale proof.

1. Why an elastic hard-sphere collision conserves momentum and energycomplete elementary proof

Claim. For unit contact normal \(\omega\in\mathbb S^{d-1}\), the collision rule

\[v_i'=v_i-((v_i-v_j)\cdot\omega)\omega,\qquad v_j'=v_j+((v_i-v_j)\cdot\omega)\omega\]

preserves total momentum and kinetic energy, is reversible, and has absolute Jacobian one.

  1. Separate center-of-mass and relative motion. Define \(V=(v_i+v_j)/2\) and \(g=v_i-v_j\). Then \(v_i=V+g/2\) and \(v_j=V-g/2\).
  2. Rewrite the collision rule. Adding the two displayed equations gives \(v_i'+v_j'=v_i+v_j\), so \(V'=V\). Subtracting them gives \(g'=g-2(g\cdot\omega)\omega\).
  3. Identify the geometry. Decompose \(g=g_{\perp}+g_{\parallel}\), where \(g_{\parallel}=(g\cdot\omega)\omega\) and \(g_{\perp}\cdot\omega=0\). Then \(g'=g_{\perp}-g_{\parallel}\): the normal component reverses and the tangential component is unchanged.
  4. Momentum conservation. Since \(V'=V\), we have \(v_i'+v_j'=2V'=2V=v_i+v_j\).
  5. Energy conservation. Orthogonality gives \(|g'|^2=|g_{\perp}|^2+|g_{\parallel}|^2=|g|^2\). Also
    \[|v_i|^2+|v_j|^2=2|V|^2+\tfrac12|g|^2.\]
    Both terms on the right are unchanged, hence so is total kinetic energy.
  6. Reversibility. Applying the reflection twice restores \(g\): \(g''=g\). Therefore the collision map is an involution.
  7. Jacobian. The reflection has eigenvalue \(-1\) along \(\omega\) and \(+1\) on \(\omega^\perp\), so its determinant is \(-1\). The center-of-mass coordinate is unchanged; therefore the full velocity map has absolute Jacobian one.
What the compressed proof hides

“It is a reflection” simultaneously proves four facts, but only after choosing the correct coordinates: center-of-mass velocity carries momentum, while the length of relative velocity carries the remaining energy.

2. How Liouville transport becomes the BBGKY boundary-flux termstep-by-step derivation; trace theory suppressed

Setup. Write \(z_i=(x_i,v_i)\), \(Z_s=(z_1,\ldots,z_s)\), and let \(F_N(t,Z_N)\) be a symmetric density on the hard-sphere domain \(\mathcal D_N=\{|x_i-x_j|\ge\varepsilon\}\). The marginal \(f_N^{(s)}\) is obtained by integrating out particles \(s+1,\ldots,N\).

  1. Start from the interior equation. Away from contact surfaces,
    \[\partial_tF_N+\sum_{m=1}^Nv_m\cdot\nabla_{x_m}F_N=0.\]
  2. Integrate over the hidden variables. Integrating the time derivative produces \(\partial_tf_N^{(s)}\). For \(m\le s\), the transport derivative remains visible and produces \(v_m\cdot\nabla_{x_m}f_N^{(s)}\).
  3. Do not discard hidden transport. For \(m>s\), integration by parts turns \(v_m\cdot\nabla_{x_m}F_N\) into flux through the boundary of the admissible fiber. The domain has a boundary because hidden particles may not overlap observed ones.
  4. Parametrize one contact boundary. When hidden particle \(s+1\) touches observed particle \(i\), write
    \[x_{s+1}=x_i+\varepsilon\omega,\qquad \omega\in\mathbb S^{d-1}.\]
    The surface element is \(dS=\varepsilon^{d-1}d\omega\).
  5. Compute normal velocity. The relative position is \(y=x_{s+1}-x_i\), so \(\dot y=v_{s+1}-v_i\). Its normal speed through \(|y|=\varepsilon\) is \((v_{s+1}-v_i)\cdot\omega\). This is the collision-rate factor.
  6. Separate entering from leaving flux. The positive part \([a]_+=\max(a,0)\) selects one orientation. The hard-sphere boundary condition identifies the opposite orientation with the elastically reflected velocities \((v_i',v_{s+1}')\). After this change of variables, outgoing minus incoming flux becomes a gain-minus-loss collision operator \(C_{i,s+1}^{\varepsilon}\).
  7. Count hidden partners. Any of the \(N-s\) hidden particles can be the particle at contact. Symmetry of \(F_N\) makes their contributions identical, giving the factor \(N-s\).
  8. Sum over observed particles. Any \(i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}\) can collide with the hidden particle. Therefore
    \[\left(\partial_t+\sum_{i=1}^sv_i\cdot\nabla_{x_i}\right)f_N^{(s)}=(N-s)\varepsilon^{d-1}\sum_{i=1}^sC_{i,s+1}^{\varepsilon}f_N^{(s+1)}.\]
Where the real rigor lives

The algebra above explains every factor. A full proof must define boundary traces of \(F_N\), handle contacts among observed particles, fix the marginal normalization, and exclude simultaneous or grazing collisions, which form singular sets for the flow.

3. Why factorization closes the Boltzmann hierarchycomplete algebraic proof; not a proof of chaos

Claim. A factorized hierarchy \(f^{(s)}(t,Z_s)=\prod_{j=1}^sf(t,z_j)\) solves the limiting Boltzmann hierarchy exactly when the one-particle factor \(f\) solves the nonlinear Boltzmann equation.

  1. Differentiate the product. Each derivative acts on one factor at a time:
    \[\left(\partial_t+\sum_{i=1}^sv_i\cdot\nabla_{x_i}\right)\prod_{j=1}^sf(z_j)=\sum_{i=1}^s\left[(\partial_t+v_i\cdot\nabla_{x_i})f(z_i)\right]\prod_{j\ne i}f(z_j).\]
  2. Insert one additional factor on the hierarchy’s right side. At level \(s+1\), factorization says \(f^{(s+1)}=f(z_{s+1})\prod_{j=1}^sf(z_j)\).
  3. Use locality of one collision. The operator \(C_{i,s+1}\) acts only on variables \(z_i,z_{s+1}\). Every factor with \(j\ne i\) is constant with respect to that operation and can be pulled outside it.
  4. Recognize the one-particle collision integral. Integrating the new particle’s velocity and collision direction gives
    \[C_{i,s+1}f^{\otimes(s+1)}=Q(f,f)(z_i)\prod_{j\ne i}f(z_j).\]
  5. Compare the two sides term by term. The hierarchy becomes
    \[\sum_{i=1}^s\left[(\partial_t+v_i\cdot\nabla_{x_i})f(z_i)\right]\prod_{j\ne i}f(z_j)=\alpha\sum_{i=1}^sQ(f,f)(z_i)\prod_{j\ne i}f(z_j).\]
  6. Close at level one. Taking \(s=1\) gives \((\partial_t+v\cdot\nabla_x)f=\alpha Q(f,f)\). Conversely, if this equation holds, substituting it into every summand proves the hierarchy at every \(s\).
Logical warning

This proves only that the factorized manifold is invariant under the limiting hierarchy. It does not prove that finite hard-sphere marginals approach that manifold. Propagation of chaos is the separate, difficult convergence theorem.

4. The H-theorem with every sign and symmetry exposedcomplete for smooth positive solutions

Assumptions. Let \(f(t,v)>0\) be a smooth spatially homogeneous solution with enough decay to justify differentiation and all changes of variables. Define \(H[f]=\int f\log f\,dv\).

  1. Differentiate.
    \[\frac{dH}{dt}=\int (1+\log f)\,\partial_tf\,dv.\]
  2. Remove the constant term. Since \(\partial_tf=Q(f,f)\) and collisions conserve mass, \(\int Q(f,f)\,dv=0\). Hence
    \[\frac{dH}{dt}=\int Q(f,f)\log f\,dv.\]
  3. Write gain minus loss. Set \(f=f(v)\), \(f_*=f(v_*)\), \(f'=f(v')\), and \(f_*'=f(v_*')\). Then the integrand contains \(B(f'f_*'-ff_*)\log f\), where \(B\ge0\) is the collision kernel.
  4. Exchange particle labels. Swapping \(v\) and \(v_*\) leaves the collision measure invariant, so averaging gives the same expression with \((\log f+\log f_*)/2\).
  5. Exchange pre- and post-collisional states. The elastic collision map has Jacobian one and preserves \(B\). Applying it changes gain into loss. Averaging the pre/post forms supplies the negative sign and yields
    \[\frac{dH}{dt}=-\frac14\iiint B\,(f'f_*'-ff_*)\big(\log f'+\log f_*'-\log f-\log f_*\big)\,d\omega\,dv_*\,dv.\]
  6. Combine logarithms. Define \(A=f'f_*'\) and \(B_0=ff_*\). The logarithmic bracket equals \(\log(A/B_0)\), so
    \[\frac{dH}{dt}=-\frac14\iiint B\,(A-B_0)\log\frac{A}{B_0}\,d\omega\,dv_*\,dv.\]
  7. Determine the sign. Because \(\log\) is increasing, \((A-B_0)(\log A-\log B_0)\ge0\). Since the remaining factors are nonnegative, \(dH/dt\le0\).
  8. Read the equality case. Vanishing entropy production forces \(f'f_*'=ff_*\) on collisional configurations. Thus \(\log f\) is a collision invariant. Under the stated regularity, collision invariants are \(a+b\cdot v+c|v|^2\); integrability forces \(c<0\), so \(f\) is Maxwellian.
Scope

For weak or renormalized solutions, the formal differentiations may be unavailable. One proves an entropy inequality by approximation and lower semicontinuity; the elementary sign mechanism is the same, but the analytical theorem is not contained in the calculation above.

5. Why Lanford’s collision-tree proof is short-time—and why naïve restarting failsresearch proof architecture, not a complete theorem proof

Question. If each fixed collision tree can be compared with Boltzmann dynamics, why does the classical argument not automatically reach arbitrary kinetic times?

  1. Iterate the hierarchy backward. A term with \(k\) collision operators describes an observed cluster with \(k\) added ancestors. At the \(r\)-th creation, roughly \(s+r-1\) existing particles can be chosen as parent.
  2. Count histories. The number of labeled parent choices therefore grows like
    \[s(s+1)\cdots(s+k-1)=\frac{(s+k-1)!}{(s-1)!}.\]
  3. Integrate ordered collision times. The simplex \(0<t_k<\cdots<t_1<t\) has volume \(t^k/k!\). This cancels much of the factorial tree count, but typical absolute estimates still leave a geometric majorant of the form \(C_s(Ct)^k\).
  4. Locate the convergence restriction. Summing over all \(k\) is then controlled only while \(Ct<1\). The constant \(C\) also contains velocity-weight and collision-kernel losses. This is the analytic source of the short interval.
  5. Use geometry for each fixed history. For a fixed finite tree, parameters producing an extra recollision occupy a small set as \(\varepsilon\to0\). Outside that set, the hard-sphere pseudo-trajectory shadows the Boltzmann one.
  6. See why fixed-tree smallness is insufficient. Long time requires summing over deeper and more numerous trees. A small error for each fixed tree is not useful unless its decay beats the total number and analytic size of all trees.
  7. Test the tempting restart. At the end of a short interval \([0,\tau]\), the finite-\(N\) law is no longer a fresh tensor product. Particles that collided carry structured correlations of size small but nonzero. Restarting the original factorized estimate at \(\tau\) silently discards precisely those correlations.
  8. State what a long-time method must add. One needs state variables that remember connected correlations and their histories, estimates stable under contraction, and a renormalized layer-to-layer evolution. In the Deng–Hani–Ma architecture, decorated cumulants and molecule/diagram reductions supply this missing bookkeeping.
Boundary between explanation and proof

These steps explain the obstruction and the design of the remedy. They do not establish the long-time theorem: the research proof must define the decorated objects, prove their norm estimates, control recollision sets uniformly across layers, and sum every error with the published hypotheses.

1. Collision reflection

Show that the hard-sphere collision map is an involution and compute its determinant in relative-velocity coordinates.

\(g'=R_\omega g=(I-2\omega\otimes\omega)g\). Since \(R_\omega^2=I\), the map is an involution. Its eigenvalues are \(-1\) in the \(\omega\) direction and \(+1\) on \(\omega^\perp\), hence \(\det R_\omega=-1\), absolute Jacobian 1.

2. Boltzmann–Grad geometry

Derive the scaling \(N\varepsilon^{d-1}=O(1)\) from collision-tube geometry and show the volume fraction vanishes.

In time \(dt\), relative motion sweeps an \(O(\varepsilon^{d-1}|g|dt)\) collision tube. Multiplication by density \(N\) gives finite collision rate only if \(N\varepsilon^{d-1}=O(1)\). Then \(N\varepsilon^d=(N\varepsilon^{d-1})\varepsilon\to0\).

3. Maxwellian moments

Compute \(\int v_i v_j M_{\rho,u,T}\,dv\).

Set \(w=v-u\). Odd Gaussian terms vanish and \(\int w_iw_jM\,dv=\rho T\delta_{ij}\). Thus \(\int v\otimes vM\,dv=\rho u\otimes u+\rho T I\).

4. H-theorem inequality

Prove \((a-b)\log(a/b)\ge0\) for \(a,b>0\).

\(\log\) is strictly increasing, so \(a-b\) and \(\log a-\log b\) have the same sign. Their product is nonnegative.

5. Cumulant independence

Using the cumulant generating function, show mixed cumulants vanish when variables split into independent subfamilies.

If \(X\) and \(Y\) are independent, \(K_{X,Y}(s,t)=\log\mathbb E e^{s\cdot X+t\cdot Y}=K_X(s)+K_Y(t)\). Any mixed derivative involving both \(s\) and \(t\) is zero at the origin.

6. Kinetic time for random NLS

Explain why the first-order perturbation does not generically change \(\mathbb E|\widehat u_k|^2\) at order \(\alpha t\).

The first-order correction is cubic in centered independent modes. Pairing it against the linear term produces an odd or nonresonantly centered Gaussian expectation after Wick expansion, so the secular contribution starts at second order. Thus the energy changes at rate \(O(\alpha^2)\).

If \(N\varepsilon^{d-1}\to\alpha\), estimate \(N\varepsilon^d\).

Simply \(N\varepsilon^d=(N\varepsilon^{d-1})\varepsilon\to\alpha\varepsilon\to0\). This is why excluded volume disappears while collision frequency remains finite.

3. Verify the weak conservation identities for Boltzmann.

Use the symmetrized weak form to prove \(\int Q(f,f)(1,v,|v|^2)\,dv=0\).

In the weak form the factor multiplying \(f'f_*'-ff_*\) is \(\phi(v)+\phi(v_*)-\phi(v')-\phi(v_*')\). For \(\phi=1\) it vanishes trivially; for \(v\) it vanishes by momentum conservation; for \(|v|^2\) by energy conservation. Therefore the integral is zero.

4. Derive Euler pressure from a Maxwellian.

Show \(\int(v-u)\otimes(v-u)M\,dv=\rho TI\).

Set \(c=(v-u)/\sqrt T\). Then \(Mdv=\rho(2\pi)^{-d/2}e^{-|c|^2/2}dc\). Odd cross moments vanish and \(\mathbb E[c_ic_j]=\delta_{ij}\). Multiplying back by \(T\) gives \(\rho T\delta_{ij}\), hence pressure \(p=\rho T\).

5. Explain why kinetic time for cubic waves is \(\alpha^{-2}\).

Give the perturbative scaling argument for the spectrum.

The amplitude changes at first order by \(O(\alpha t)\), but centered random phases make the leading change in the quadratic observable \(\mathbb E|a_k|^2\) arise at second order. Secular second-order terms scale like \(\alpha^2t\). An order-one spectral change therefore occurs when \(t\sim\alpha^{-2}\).

6. Why does a factorized hierarchy produce a nonlinear equation?

Make the algebra explicit.

The hierarchy is linear in \(f^{(s)}\) but couples level \(s\) to \(s+1\). Substituting \(f^{(s+1)}=f^{\otimes(s+1)}\), the collision operator integrates out one copy of \(f\) while retaining another, producing \(Q(f,f)\), quadratic in the single unknown \(f\). Closure converts linear inter-level coupling into nonlinearity.

7. Diagnose why restarting Lanford is invalid.

State the missing hypothesis after the first time interval.

After one kinetic interval the exact hard-sphere ensemble has collision-generated connected correlations. Even though fixed-order correlations may be small as \(\varepsilon\to0\), their structured history is not equivalent to a fresh factorized initial ensemble. A restart theorem would need hypotheses stable under this accumulated dependence. The long-time cumulant ansatz is designed precisely to provide such stable hypotheses.
