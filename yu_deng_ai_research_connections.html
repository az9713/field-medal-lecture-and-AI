Research memo · mathematics first

Can Yu Deng’s mathematics illuminate AI theory?

Yes—but through proof technology, not metaphor. The credible bridge is the control of large interacting systems when weak correlations accumulate over long times. The strongest target is finite-width neural-network training beyond the frozen-kernel regime.

01 · What survives scrutiny

The bridge is structural, not literal.

Deng’s recurring problem is to begin with a huge deterministic or random microscopic system, identify a simpler statistical evolution, and prove that neglected correlations stay controlled on the time scale of interest. AI theory repeatedly encounters the same abstract obstruction.

Established on both sides

There is a common mathematical skeleton

  1. Microscopic state: many particles, Fourier modes, neurons, or token representations interact.
  2. Macroscopic description: replace the finite system by a probability distribution, kernel, or continuum equation.
  3. Closure: approximate high-order joint information using low-order statistics.
  4. Failure mechanism: small correlations generated at each interaction can accumulate with time, depth, or training steps.
  5. Proof challenge: organize interaction histories finely enough to show which connected correlations matter and which cancel or remain small.
Boundary of the claim. This shared skeleton suggests reusable proof architecture. It does not identify a physical collision operator, a Boltzmann entropy, or a hydrodynamic limit inside a modern language model.

03 · Connection one

Wide networks already look like particle systems.

Established AI theory

For a two-layer network of width \(m\), write each neuron’s trainable parameters as \(\theta_i=(a_i,w_i)\), where \(a_i\) is its output weight and \(w_i\) its input-weight vector:

\[ f_m(x)=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_i\,\sigma(w_i\!\cdot x), \qquad \rho_t^m=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\delta_{\theta_i(t)}. \]The empirical measure \(\rho_t^m\) records the population of neurons in parameter space. A single neuron is a “particle” only in this formal sense.

Under suitable scaling and assumptions, the large-width limit is described by a nonlinear transport equation for a probability measure \(\rho_t\):

\[ \partial_t\rho_t+\nabla_\theta\!\cdot\bigl(\rho_t V[\rho_t]\bigr)=0. \]The velocity field \(V[\rho_t]\) depends on the whole current population, because every neuron contributes to the network prediction and hence to every other neuron’s gradient.

This is a genuine interacting-particle/mean-field structure, proved in the two-layer setting by Mei, Montanari, and Nguyen and developed widely since. But standard stability bounds often have the schematic form

\[ \mathbb E\,W_2(\rho_t^m,\rho_t)\ \lesssim\ \frac{e^{Ct}}{\sqrt m}. \]\(W_2\) is the quadratic Wasserstein distance. Even if the initial sampling error is \(m^{-1/2}\), an exponential stability factor can make the bound useless when \(t\) grows with width.
Where Deng may inspire

The opportunity is not to import the Boltzmann equation. It is to replace a crude global Grönwall estimate by a structured expansion of the interaction history, retaining only the connected neuron correlations that can actually propagate error.

Important limitation. Uniform-in-time propagation of chaos is already known for some neural-training models under strong stabilizing assumptions—convexity, hypocoercivity, functional inequalities, or sufficient loss decay. A Deng-style program is interesting chiefly where those contraction mechanisms fail: nonconvex feature learning, order-one representation change, or weight sharing.
04 · Strongest connection

The neural tangent hierarchy is AI’s closest BBGKY analogue.

During gradient flow, the network outputs are driven by a time-dependent tangent kernel. That kernel is driven by a higher-order kernel, and so on—an exact hierarchy before any closure approximation.

Exact hierarchy; notation schematic

On training inputs \(x_1,\dots,x_n\), let \(f_\alpha(t)=f(x_\alpha;\theta(t))\), targets be \(y_\alpha\), and \(K_t^{(2)}\) be the neural tangent kernel. For squared loss, output dynamics take the form

\[ \dot f_\alpha(t)=-\frac1n\sum_{\beta=1}^{n}K_t^{(2)}(x_\alpha,x_\beta)\bigl(f_\beta(t)-y_\beta\bigr). \]If \(K_t^{(2)}\) were frozen, this would be a linear kernel regression flow. Finite-width feature learning appears through the evolution of \(K_t^{(2)}\).

Differentiating the kernel introduces a third-order object; differentiating that introduces a fourth-order object:

\[ \partial_tK_t^{(r)}(x_{\alpha_1},\ldots,x_{\alpha_r})=-\frac1n\sum_{\beta=1}^{n}K_t^{(r+1)}(x_{\alpha_1},\ldots,x_{\alpha_r},x_\beta)\bigl(f_\beta-y_\beta\bigr). \]Precise definitions and architecture-dependent prefactors are given in the Neural Tangent Hierarchy literature. The structural fact is the coupling \(r\mapsto r+1\).

Proposed closure variable

Instead of controlling raw \(K^{(r)}\) separately, define connected components \(C_t^{(r)}\) by subtracting all products of lower-order pieces, just as joint cumulants subtract correlations explained by partitions. One would seek an evolution of the schematic form

\[ \partial_t C_t^{(r)}=\mathcal L_t C_t^{(r)}+\mathcal A_t C_t^{(r+1)}+\mathcal N_r\!\left(C_t^{(2)},\ldots,C_t^{(r)}\right), \qquad \|C_t^{(r)}\|\le m^{1-r/2}\Phi_r(t). \]\(\mathcal L_t\) is the linearized training flow, \(\mathcal A_t\) couples to the next level, and \(\mathcal N_r\) collects lower-order nonlinear combinations. This is a research template, not a proved theorem.

The hoped-for gain is conceptual: disconnected contributions may be large individually but already encoded by lower-order quantities. The genuinely new information is carried by connected diagrams. Deng’s long-time cumulant method is precisely designed around that distinction.

05 · Connection two

Transformer depth admits a continuum picture—with severe caveats.

Established in stylized transformer models

Represent \(n\) token states at layer \(\ell\) by \(x_1^\ell,\ldots,x_n^\ell\), and their empirical distribution by \(\mu_\ell^n=n^{-1}\sum_j\delta_{x_j^\ell}\). A residual attention update can be idealized as

\[ x_i^{\ell+1}=x_i^\ell+\eta\,V[\mu_\ell^n](x_i^\ell), \qquad \mu_\ell^n=\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n\delta_{x_j^\ell}. \]The field \(V\) represents aggregation through attention. Taking residual step size \(\eta\to0\) and depth \(\ell\to\infty\) with \(t=\ell\eta\) fixed leads formally—and for several specified attention models rigorously—to a measure-valued transport PDE.
\[ \partial_t\mu_t+\nabla_x\!\cdot\bigl(\mu_tV[\mu_t]\bigr)=0. \]
Possible research direction

Finite-token corrections could be organized by connected token cumulants and interaction diagrams across depth. This might improve bounds whose naïve stability factor grows like \(e^{Lt}\), especially when attention repeatedly reuses the same representations.

Why the gas analogy breaks. Attention is dense and global, not dilute and binary. Real tokens are neither independent nor generally exchangeable. Positional encoding, causal masks, semantics, and learned weights break symmetries. Attention can create order-one correlations in one layer. Therefore only the hierarchy-and-cumulant philosophy is portable; hard-sphere estimates are not.
06 · Connection three

Random tensors may be the most direct methodological bridge.

Deng, Nahmod, and Yue control multilinear Gaussian expansions whose random tensor coefficients are adapted to previously revealed randomness. Neural networks have exactly this dependence problem: the same random weights are reused across layers, paths, and training steps.

Shared algebraic object
\[ X_i=\sum_{j_1,\ldots,j_r}T_{i;j_1\ldots j_r}\,g_{j_1}\cdots g_{j_r}. \]The \(g_j\) are Gaussian variables. The tensor \(T\) may itself depend on part of the randomness, so a black-box independence estimate is invalid. “Adapted” means that dependence is exposed and controlled in a filtration-compatible order.

Tensor-program theory rigorously identifies leading infinite-width Gaussian-process limits for broad architectures. Neural-tangent hierarchies and Feynman-diagram calculations describe finite-width corrections in selected regimes. The open gap is a robust, nonperturbative calculus that remains stable under many layers or training steps while tracking weight sharing and feature learning.

Credible targets
Weight-shared transformers. The same parameter matrices enter many token paths, creating structured dependence that simple Gaussian independence cannot see.
Recurrent networks. Reusing weights over time produces adapted random coefficients naturally indexed by computational history.
Mixture-of-experts routing. Random routing and learned gates induce sparse but history-dependent tensor contractions.
Finite-width feature learning. Parameters become correlated with their own initialization through training, invalidating static random-feature heuristics.
07 · A sharper stochastic connection

Diffusion models and Langevin dynamics are not the same case.

Langevin training gives a genuine interacting-particle hierarchy. A standard pretrained diffusion model usually does not: conditional on its learned score, independently generated samples do not interact. The Deng connection becomes substantial only when the drift depends on the empirical population, or when one studies many noisy neurons during training.

Case A · Standard score-based diffusion

Forward noise is easy; reverse-time reconstruction is learned.

A continuous-time score-based model starts from data \(X_0\sim p_0\) and adds noise through the Itô SDE

\[ dX_t=b(X_t,t)\,dt+g(t)\,dW_t, \qquad 0\le t\le T. \]\(W_t\) is Brownian motion, \(b\) is a prescribed drift, and \(g(t)\) is the noise amplitude. The marginal density \(p_t\) becomes progressively smoother and is designed to approach a simple prior at time \(T\).

Running time backward requires the score \(s_t(x)=\nabla_x\log p_t(x)\). With time parametrized in the reverse direction, the reverse SDE is conventionally written

\[ dX_t=\bigl[b(X_t,t)-g(t)^2\nabla_x\log p_t(X_t)\bigr]dt+g(t)\,d\overline W_t, \qquad dt<0. \]The sign convention matters: \(dt<0\) indicates integration from \(T\) down to \(0\). A neural network \(s_\theta(x,t)\) approximates the unknown score. Sampling error therefore comes from score error, time discretization, and stochastic integration.
Why Deng’s machinery is not automatically relevant. Once \(s_\theta\) is fixed, two generated samples obey independent copies of this SDE. Their joint law factorizes exactly if their initial noises are independent. There is no BBGKY closure problem and no collision-generated cumulant hierarchy to control.
Case B · Mean-field Langevin neural training

Here the particles really interact.

Let \(\theta_i\) denote neuron \(i\)’s parameters and \(\rho_t^m=m^{-1}\sum_i\delta_{\theta_i(t)}\) their empirical law. A noisy mean-field training model has the schematic form

\[ d\theta_i(t)=-\nabla_\theta\frac{\delta\mathcal R}{\delta\rho}\!\left(\rho_t^m\right)(\theta_i(t))\,dt+\sqrt{2\beta^{-1}}\,dB_i(t). \]\(\mathcal R(\rho)\) is the population risk functional, \(\delta\mathcal R/\delta\rho\) its first variation, \(B_i\) represents independent Brownian motions, and \(\beta\) is the inverse noise level. Every particle feels the empirical distribution, so the neurons are coupled.

As \(m\to\infty\), the one-particle law can satisfy a nonlinear Fokker–Planck equation

\[ \partial_t\rho_t=\nabla_\theta\!\cdot\!\left(\rho_t\nabla_\theta\frac{\delta\mathcal R}{\delta\rho}(\rho_t)\right)+\beta^{-1}\Delta_\theta\rho_t. \]The first term transports neurons down the population-risk gradient; the Laplacian diffuses the distribution. The associated free energy is typically risk plus an entropy regularizer, under the hypotheses of the particular model.

The finite-\(m\) joint law has a hierarchy of marginals, and “propagation of chaos” asks whether any fixed number of neurons become asymptotically independent with common law \(\rho_t\). Uniform-in-time results already exist under functional convexity, hypocoercivity, log-Sobolev, or related stabilizing assumptions.

Where Deng may add value

The open opportunity is the noncontractive regime: nonconvex risk, metastable wells, weak or vanishing noise, feature learning, or times that grow with width. A connected-cumulant hierarchy could try to exploit three mechanisms simultaneously:

  1. Diffusive smoothing: Brownian noise regularizes short scales and may damp some high-order correlations.
  2. Connected-history counting: only interaction graphs that genuinely link the observed neurons contribute to their cumulant.
  3. Late-time dissipation: decay of free energy or training residual can make the cumulative forcing integrable even without global convexity.
\[ \sup_{t\ge0}\left\|\operatorname{Cum}_r\!\left(\theta_{1}(t),\ldots,\theta_r(t)\right)\right\|\ \stackrel{?}{\lesssim}\ m^{1-r}\,C_r(\beta,\mathcal R). \]This is a proposed target, not a known bound in this generality. The exponent and norm depend on normalization, observables, and assumptions. Its purpose is to state what “long-time propagation of randomness” would have to mean quantitatively.
Case C · Interacting diffusion generators

Diffusion models can be redesigned to create the missing hierarchy.

Some generative models evolve a set \(X_t^1,\ldots,X_t^N\) jointly, with drift depending on the empirical measure \(\mu_t^N=N^{-1}\sum_j\delta_{X_t^j}\):

\[ dX_t^i=B\!\left(X_t^i,\mu_t^N,t\right)dt+\sigma(t)dW_t^i. \]This is a McKean–Vlasov particle system. Its large-\(N\) density solves a nonlinear Fokker–Planck equation, and finite-\(N\) accuracy is genuinely a propagation-of-chaos problem. Mean-field chaos diffusion models are one explicit AI proposal in this direction.

Here a Deng-inspired program is logically coherent: track connected sample correlations across the reverse diffusion, organize long interaction histories, and seek error bounds uniform in sampling time or stable under aggressive discretization. But the diagrams would encode drift derivatives and Gaussian noise pairings—not hard-sphere collision molecules.

08 · Best research bet

A connected neural tangent hierarchy for long-time feature learning.

Falsifiable proposed theorem program
For a specified finite-width network and training distribution, prove that a truncated hierarchy of connected tangent correlations approximates gradient-flow training until convergence, with error \(O(m^{-\gamma})\) for some \(\gamma>0\), while permitting order-one evolution of the neural tangent kernel.

This statement is intentionally a target, not a claim of existing proof. “Specified” must include architecture, initialization, data distribution, loss, normalization, width scaling, and the norm in which approximation is measured.

01

Choose a tractable model

Start with a two-layer or residual network where the exact hierarchy is explicit and training residuals can be controlled.

02

Define connected objects

Use a partition lattice or generating functional to remove all lower-order factorizations from each tangent correlation.

03

Expand only locally in time

Avoid a single global series. Restart expansions on time slabs while carrying forward controlled cumulant data—the conceptual role of partial time expansion.

04

Classify histories

Encode derivative contractions as diagrams, cut them at articulation structures, and prove summability of the dangerous connected classes.

05

Exploit optimization decay

The driving factor \(f(t)-y\) may decay. Integrability of the residual can replace unavailable contractivity and suppress late-time accumulation.

06

Prove a non-NTK phenomenon

The result matters only if it allows \(K_t^{(2)}-K_0^{(2)}=O(1)\). Otherwise existing lazy-training theory may already suffice.

09 · Epistemic guardrails

How to know whether the analogy earns its keep.

Pass

Concrete hierarchy

The AI model has an exact or controlled hierarchy whose order \(r\) object couples to order \(r+1\).

Pass

Connectedness gives a bound

Subtracting factorizable pieces yields extra powers of width, cancellation, or temporal integrability.

Pass

Long-time improvement

The method improves a bound on a time or depth scale where direct Grönwall or fixed-order truncation fails.

Fail

Vocabulary-only transfer

Renaming neurons “particles” or training steps “collisions” produces no new estimate or theorem.

Fail

Missing small parameter

No width, interaction, noise, or scaling parameter controls the proposed hierarchy; “correlation is small” is merely asserted.

Fail

False physical identification

Claims that loss is Boltzmann entropy, intelligence is a hydrodynamic limit, or scaling laws are Boltzmann–Grad lack defined operators and hypotheses.
